PRELIMINARY NOTE
Purpose and structure
In brief: What the sector edition of the evidence kit is, what it is not, in
which order it is filled in, and how you can tell it is finished.
This kit is for three professions in which confidentiality is not one rule among many but the foundation of the
profession: the law firm (Rechtsanwaltskanzlei), the tax advisory firm (Steuerberatungskanzlei) and the
medical practice (Ordination). It takes the five records of the general AI evidence kit — usage policy, tool
register with approval form, training record, labelling checklist, entry-into-force plan — and sharpens them to
what is different in these professions: which data an assistant must never see there, how the register and
the approval check treat these types of data, who is bound by confidentiality, and which questions the
business clarifies with its chamber or a professional before use. Three files are added that the general kit
does not have: a supplement sheet to the policy per profession, a page “What never goes in here” per
profession, and a chamber checklist.
What the kit is
A set of forms in which the passages where the business has to decide are highlighted in yellow and set in
square brackets. Next to each of these passages stands one sentence in italics: what the decision means
and where in the handbook “AI Policies for the Workforce” (Volume 2 of the series) or in this guide the basis is
given. That is the rule which sets this kit apart from a collection of templates: every gap is marked and
explained. A template in which you only enter the name of the firm decides, in the business’s place, things it
cannot see — and in these three professions those are things for which the licensed professional is
personally answerable.
The sector edition is self-contained: whoever has it does not need the general kit alongside. Whoever has
already filled in the general kit will recognise its files and will find, for each file in this guide, the section “What
is different compared with the general kit”.
What the kit is not
It is not legal advice and not a legal opinion. And in this edition a second sentence applies that matters even
more: The kit never says what confidentiality permits. Whether entering the facts of a case into an AI tool
is a disclosure, whether a contract with the provider suffices, whether the client or the patient must be
informed, whether an anonymised input is permissible — no file in this kit answers any of these questions.
The files record what the business has decided and name the place where it has this checked: the chamber
or a professional. The chamber checklist is therefore not an enclosure but the first step of the plan.
Nor does the kit establish any legal position anew. The legal bases of confidentiality that appear in the files
as examples — the Rechtsanwaltsordnung (Lawyers' Code), the Wirtschaftstreuhandberufsgesetz 2017
(Public Accountants' Act), the Ärztegesetz 1998 (Physicians' Act) — come from search extracts that could
not be read in the original for this kit; this is stated in every file that names them and in the annex. The
business reads the provision in the Federal Legal Information System and enters version and date. What this
guide says about the publications of the chambers, it says with a date and with the note that they were not
read.
The kit contains no names of tools or providers. It supplies the empty register and the procedure by which
the business keeps it itself, with a date in every row.

The three professions — and who else
The files speak of the “licensed professional” (Berufsträgerin oder Berufsträger) and mean the person who
carries confidentiality under professional law: the lawyer, the tax advisor, the physician. In the three
professions that is almost always also the management, and so this person appears in the files where the
general kit says “management”. Notaries, pharmacies, psychotherapy and other professions bound by
confidentiality are not the subject of this kit; the structure probably fits there too, but the legal bases and
chambers are different and were not researched.
The market of this kit is the Union; the examples come from Austria and are marked as such. Professional
law is national. What the kit names as legal bases and chambers applies to Austria; a business in another
Member State fills the same fields with its own provisions and its own chamber and puts the same questions
to the chamber. The guide mentions at one place what is said to be regulated differently in Germany — as a
hint, not as a survey.
The order
1. Open the chamber checklist (kammer-pruefliste-en.docx) and open the entry-into-force plan
(inkraftsetzung-en.xlsx). The checklist is step 2 of the plan, and it belongs at the start, because the
answers there decide which tools are eligible for which data at all. The questions are asked before a tool
is procured or an account is set up.
2. Fill in the supplement sheet of the profession (ergaenzung-kanzlei-en.docx, -steuerberatung-
en.docx or -ordination-en.docx). Read the legal basis in the original, chamber, auxiliary persons, the
rule for data under professional secrecy, informing clients or patients, and the table of clarifications.
3. Fill in the usage policy (nutzungsrichtlinie-en.docx). The section “Professional secrecy and
confidentiality” and the nine areas of regulation.
4. Tick, delete and add on the page “What never goes in here” (nie-hinein-<profession>-en.docx, the
same page as PDF) and post it.
5. Create the tool register (tool-register-en.xlsx, freigabeformular-en.docx). Every tool one row,
including the one in the practice software and the one on the private phone; for data under professional
secrecy, rows 8 to 10 of the approval check and the clarification.
6. Hold the briefing and record it (schulungsnachweis-en.docx, schulungsnachweis-en.xlsx) — with
every person who has access to data under professional secrecy, even if they use no tool.
7. Create the labelling checklist (kennzeichnungs-checkliste-en.docx); point 10 is always completed,
even if the business publishes nothing.
8. Complete the entry into force. Tick the last rows, enter the follow-up — with the fifth trigger that exists
only in this edition: a publication by the chamber.
How you can tell it is finished
The kit is finished when a folder — on paper or as a file store — contains the records that together answer two
questions: What have we regulated, since when, for which tools, and who knows about it? and, new in this
edition: Whom did we ask, when, and what was the answer?
The usage policy with effective date, version line and signature; no yellow passage left open.
The supplement sheet of the profession with the legal basis read, the rule for data under professional
secrecy and at least one row in the table of clarifications that carries date and evidence.
The page “What never goes in here”, ticked and edited, posted.
The tool register, in which every tool has a row with review date, the column “professional secrecy
affected” is completed, and an approval form is filed for every approved tool.
The training record with one signature per person and the date of their confidentiality declaration.

The labelling checklist with point 10 — or a dated note that nothing is currently published and no system
speaks directly to persons.
The chamber checklist, on which every row carries a date and evidence, including those answered “this
is not settled”.
The entry-into-force plan, in which every one of the eighteen rows carries a date and the follow-up is
entered.
The three example businesses
The examples in this guide refer to three fictitious businesses in Austria. People appear by their role, never by
name; the businesses do not exist.
The law firm: two lawyers, one trainee lawyer (Konzipientin), three office staff, one external IT contractor;
focus on contract and tenancy law, occasionally family matters. One lawyer has for months been using a text-
assistant tool through a private account for summaries of court decisions and first drafts; one member of the
office staff has client letters “polished” — with names.
The tax advisory firm: one tax advisor as owner, five staff in bookkeeping and payroll, one office manager,
two apprentices, one external IT contractor. The office manager uses a text-assistant tool through a
business account for client letters about deadlines; one apprentice has a tool on his phone with which he has
receipts “explained”.
The medical practice: one general practitioner, two practice assistants, one intern, one cleaner, practice
software from a provider with a maintenance contract. The physician dictates letters into a transcription app
on her private phone; one assistant has patient information on the flu vaccination worded by a text-assistant
tool — without personal data.
None of the three businesses has an IT department, none has appointed a data protection officer. In all three,
something is happening that the policy must regulate before anyone asks.
Choice of words
The files and this guide say tool; the handbook says the same thing at the same places. What is meant is an
AI system that the business uses as deployer and did not develop itself. Professional secrecy and
confidentiality are used side by side: confidentiality is the person’s duty, professional secrecy is what it
protects; the files say “data under professional secrecy” for everything the profession’s duty of
confidentiality covers. Auxiliary persons (Hilfspersonen) is the word of the Physicians' Act and means here
everyone who has access to such data in a firm or practice without being a licensed professional. Chamber
(Kammer) means the statutory professional body responsible for the business; where it gives no
information, a professional takes its place. Record means a sheet that shows, with date and signature, that
something was regulated, reviewed, asked or carried out — not that it is legally sufficient.

SECTION 1
Professional secrecy and confidentiality —
what the kit records and what it does not
In brief: The three duties of confidentiality, whom they cover, what the
chambers have published on the subject — and the line this kit does not
cross.
In an electrical business the question of what may go into an AI tool is a question of caution: calculations are
trade secrets, customer data are personal, both are better left out. In a law firm or medical practice the same
question is a question of the profession. The lawyer, the tax advisor and the physician are bound to
confidentiality by the professional law of their country; the duty is the reason people entrust them with
anything, and according to the wording of the provisions it covers not only the licensed professionals but
also their auxiliary persons. An AI tool of a third-party provider is a third party. Whether an input there is a
disclosure, under which conditions it is not, and what a contract with the provider changes — these are the
questions this kit revolves around, and it answers none of them. It records what the business has decided
and names the place where it has this checked.
1.1 The three legal bases — as examples, not as information
For the three professions the files each name one provision as an example for the gap “legal basis of
confidentiality in our profession”. All three come from search extracts: the Federal Legal Information System
and the chambers' pages were not reachable from the working environment in which this kit was produced.
The wordings agree across several independent extracts; which version applies and which paragraphs
belong to it has not been established. Therefore every file that names one of the three provisions carries the
addition “verify version against the original” — and the business enters version and date after reading.
Law firm. According to the search extract, the first sentence of the second paragraph reads: the lawyer is
bound to confidentiality about the matters entrusted to them and the facts that otherwise became known to
them in their professional capacity, the secrecy of which is in the interest of their party. The same paragraph
is said — according to a summary, not the wording — to cover shareholders and supervisory bodies of a law
firm company and to oblige the lawyer to bind them and their auxiliary staff to confidentiality and to take
sufficient precautions for compliance.
Source: § 9(2) Rechtsanwaltsordnung (RAO, Austrian Lawyers' Code), RGBl. No. 96/1868 as amended; wording from a search extract, not
read in the original, version not established; official reference ris.bka.gv.at, consolidated federal law (retrieved 15 September 2026); check
current status.
Tax advisory firm. According to the search extract, the first paragraph reads: licensed professionals are
bound to confidentiality about the matters entrusted to them. Which paragraph or which other provision
covers the office staff or requires their obligation has not been established; what is known is that the
Chamber of Tax Advisors and Auditors provides a template “Erklärung zur Verschwiegenheitspflicht”
(confidentiality declaration) for office staff.
Source: § 80(1) Wirtschaftstreuhandberufsgesetz 2017 (WTBG 2017, Austrian Public Accountants' Act), BGBl. I No. 137/2017 as amended;
wording from a search extract, not read in the original; official reference ris.bka.gv.at (retrieved 15 September 2026); check current status.
Chamber template: ksw.or.at, regional office Upper Austria, file “Verschwiegenheit_.doc”, existence from a search extract, content not read
(retrieved 15 September 2026).
Medical practice. According to the search extract, the first paragraph reads: the physician and their auxiliary
persons are bound to confidentiality about all secrets entrusted to them or that became known to them in
the exercise of their profession. This is the only one of the three provisions that names auxiliary persons in

its first sentence itself. In addition, what applies to all three but concerns every row in the practice: health
data are a special category of personal data whose processing is prohibited under the General Data
Protection Regulation unless one of the exceptions applies — among them care and treatment by
professional staff subject to professional secrecy or by another person who is likewise subject to an
obligation of secrecy. Whether an AI provider can be such another person, this kit does not say; the
handbook “AI in the Company” (Volume 1) covers the data protection side in section 11.
Source: § 54(1) Ärztegesetz 1998 (ÄrzteG 1998, Austrian Physicians' Act), BGBl. I No. 169/1998 as amended; wording from a search extract,
not read in the original, version not established; official reference ris.bka.gv.at/eli/bgbl/i/1998/169/P54 (retrieved 15 September 2026); check

WHAT THIS KIT DOES NOT DERIVE FROM THE THREE PROVISIONS
It does not derive that an input into an AI tool is a disclosure. It does not derive that it is
not. It does not derive that a data processing agreement suffices, that a contract with a
secrecy clause suffices, or that an anonymised input is permissible. Each of these
statements would be legal advice, and each would depend on a provision that was not
read in the original for this kit. The files have a gap at each of these places, and the
chamber checklist has the question.
1.2 Whom confidentiality covers — and why that changes the training list
All three provisions cover auxiliary persons according to the search extracts: the practice assistant, the
office clerk, the trainee lawyer, the apprentice in bookkeeping. The medical chambers advise, according to
the same extracts, to inform auxiliary staff in writing about the duty of confidentiality and to file the signed
copy in the personnel file; the tax advisors' chamber provides a template. For the criminal provision that
protects professional secrecy in the health sector, auxiliary staff and persons in training are said, according
to the search extract, to be treated in the same way as the professionals — the kit names this provision in no
file, because it names no penalty as an assertion; the annex states that it exists.
For the kit a simple rule follows, set out in section 5: Every person who has access to data under
professional secrecy belongs on the training record — even if they use no AI tool. The cleaner who is
alone in the treatment room in the evening, the intern, the external IT contractor with remote access.
Whether they use a tool decides which parts of the briefing concern them; whether they belong on the list is
decided by access. The confidentiality declaration itself is not a sheet of this kit — there are chamber
templates — but the overview per person records when it was signed.
Source: medical chambers of Tyrol, Carinthia and Upper Austria, pages “Ärztliche Verschwiegenheitspflicht” (medical confidentiality), from a
search extract, not read (retrieved 15 September 2026); § 121(4) Strafgesetzbuch (Criminal Code) from a search extract in English rendering,
German wording not read (retrieved 15 September 2026); check current status.
1.3 What the chambers have published on AI — with date, without content
The kit names chamber publications with title and date and says in every file that it has not read them. That is
not negligence but the only honest form: passing off a second-hand summary as the content of a binding
professional rule would be worse than the admission.

firms) of the Österreichischer Rechtsanwaltskammertag, updated in September 2025 according to the
search extract. It is said to contain a checklist that can be presented to AI providers for written confirmation
of professional requirements, and to state that responsibility for client data and for checking results remains
with the lawyer. Whether it is a recommendation or a binding rule has not been established. In addition, there
is a conduct rule that matters for the approval check: § 40(3) of the Richtlinien für die Ausübung des

1.4 The data protection authority — and the note on other Member States
The Austrian data protection authority has published questions and answers on AI and data protection;
according to summaries it says that entering personal data into a generative AI system is processing, that
input, output and transmission to the provider are subject to the General Data Protection Regulation, and
that accuracy is a challenge with generative text systems. Whether it addresses professionals bound by
secrecy has not been established. The chamber checklist has question 10 for this.
Source: Datenschutzbehörde (Austria), “FAQ zum Thema KI und Datenschutz”, dsb.gv.at, from summaries in search extracts, not read
(retrieved 15 September 2026); check current status.
Professional law is national. A business in Germany would enter different provisions in the gap “legal basis”;
according to search extracts, the inclusion of “participating persons” — IT service providers, AI providers — in
professional secrecy is said to have been expressly regulated there since 2017 under conditions, and the
Federal Bar and the Federal Chamber of Tax Advisors are said to have published their own guidelines and
question catalogues on AI, the latter with status of 27 January 2026. None of this was read, none of it is
contained in the kit as a requirement. It only shows why the fields of the supplement sheet are delivered
empty: the business fills them with its own law and its own chamber.
Source: Bundessteuerberaterkammer (Germany), “FAQ: KI im steuerberatenden Berufsstand”, status 27 January 2026, bstbk.de, existence
and date from a search extract, not read (retrieved 15 September 2026); note, not a survey.

1.5 The line in one sentence
The general kit says: every gap is marked and explained. This edition adds: every question to professional
law is marked and passed on to the chamber. Whoever fills in the files takes decisions; whoever fills in the
chamber checklist shows that they asked before the decisions. Both together are the evidence a business
bound by professional secrecy needs — and this kit claims no more.

SECTION 2
The usage policy and the supplement sheets
In brief: The main document with the new section “Professional secrecy and
confidentiality”, the nine sharpened areas of regulation — and, per
profession, a sheet that records what has been clarified.
The usage policy is the written form of decisions the licensed professional has taken beforehand. The
handbook says it in one sentence: whoever formulates a policy before deciding writes down wishes. In the
three professions a second order is added: whoever decides before asking decides about something that
does not belong to them. That is why, in this edition, the supplement sheet comes before the policy, and the
chamber checklist before the supplement sheet.
Source: Volume 2 “AI Policies for the Workforce”, sections 4 and 7; reference, no new evidence.
2.1 What is different compared with the general kit
The file nutzungsrichtlinie-en.docx has the same nine areas of regulation in the same order as the
general kit; the order comes from section 7 of the handbook, and whoever lays both kits side by side finds
every section again. Six things are different.
A section before § 1: “Professional secrecy and confidentiality”. It has two gaps. The first is the legal basis
— with the three provisions from section 1 of this guide as examples and the addition that the version is to be
verified against the original. The second is the business’s rule for data under professional secrecy, with two
suggested answers: never, or only in tools expressly approved in the register for this type of data, after
documented clarification with the chamber or a professional. The kit prefers neither. It only says what the
second costs: per tool the approval check with rows 8 to 10 and one row in the table of clarifications with date
and evidence.
The header asks for the profession. One policy for all three professions, with a gap “profession” and the
reference to the supplement sheet of the chosen profession. Three policies instead of one would have fewer
cross-references and the same text three times; this edition chooses the one with the supplement sheet
beside it.
§ 1 names the type of data next to the tool. A tool is not “approved” but approved for the types of data listed
in the register. And private accounts are a gap pre-filled with “not permitted / permitted only for tasks without
data under professional secrecy”. In the law firm example this is the case of the lawyer with the private
account: a private account is an access the business has neither bound by contract nor configured; for
summaries of published decisions that may be tolerable, for the first draft of a submission with the facts of
the case it is the case the chamber checklist takes up first. Tools built into the practice software are
expressly in the register — they are the ones nobody set up.
Source: Data Protection Conference (Germany), Orientierungshilfe Künstliche Intelligenz und Datenschutz, version of 6 May 2024, section
2.4, pages 10 and 11, datenschutzkonferenz-online.de (read for Volume 2, retrieved 8 September 2026); Volume 2, section 6.
§ 2 excludes the professional assessment. Besides the case from Annex III of the AI Act — no final decisions
about persons — the prohibited uses have a second fixed sentence: not as a substitute for the professional’s
own assessment of a case, a filing or a finding. The kit says this as a description of the profession, not as a
rule of law; the bar’s leaflet is reported to state it expressly for law firms (not read). The permitted uses are
pre-filled with examples that manage without personal data: drafts of general letters, summaries of

<!-- Seite 13 -->
published texts, translations, wording help for website and notices. The tax advisory firm in the example
enters: explanations of deadlines and procedures without client reference, drafts for the newsletter,
translations for two foreign-language clients — the latter only with placeholders.
Source: Annex III No. 4 Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, consolidated version 02024R1689 — EN — 27.07.2026 — 001.001, eur-lex.europa.eu (read
for Volume 2, retrieved 8 September 2026); Volume 2, sections 3 and 7.
§ 3 refers to the profession’s page. Instead of a general list the section says: everything ticked on the page
“What never goes in here” for our profession — plus the types of data that occur in every business. And it has
the sentence that appears at several places in this edition: removing names is not enough where the
reference can be reconstructed from the context. Whether an anonymised input is compatible with
confidentiality the kit does not say; the German Data Protection Conference regularly considers removing
names and addresses insufficient, and for confidentiality it is question 7 on the chamber checklist.
Source: Data Protection Conference, Orientierungshilfe of 6 May 2024, section 3.1 (retrieved 8 September 2026); Volume 2, sections 1, 7 and
12.
§ 4 has a second gap for health data. They occur not only in the medical practice — also in the tax advisor’s
payroll accounting (sick leave) and in a law firm’s family or employment cases. The gap refers to Volume 1,
section 11.
§ 6, § 7 and § 8 are worded close to the professions. Release of documents leaving the business names
client letter, submission, explanation of an assessment notice, physician’s letter and patient information and
places them, in the pre-filled text, on the licensed professional, because release there coincides with the
professional duty to stand behind one’s own work. § 7 is called “Labelling and information” and separates
labelling under Art. 50 of the AI Act from the question whether clients or patients must be informed about the
use — the second is a gap that takes in the result of the chamber clarification (section 6 of this guide). § 8
has, besides the internal reporting channel, a gap for who decides on external steps after an incident and
whom they ask first: chamber, professional, insurer. Whether an incident must be reported and to whom the
kit does not say.
The review has a fifth trigger. Besides new tool, changed provider terms and changed legal situation: as
soon as the chamber publishes a recommendation or rule on the use of AI. In the three professions that is the
most likely trigger, and the one a business without a follow-up misses.
2.2 The gaps of the policy, counted
Twenty-nine yellow passages: five in the header (business, profession, effective date, version, responsible),
one in the scope, two in the professional-secrecy section, one to three in each of the nine sections, four at
the end. Each has below it the italic sentence with the basis — for the sections from the general kit it is
Volume 2 with section number, for the new passages it is this guide with section number and, where it fits,
the number of the question on the chamber checklist.
2.3 The supplement sheet per profession
The three files ergaenzung-kanzlei-en.docx, ergaenzung-steuerberatung-en.docx and ergaenzung-
ordination-en.docx have the same structure with six sections; only the pre-filled entries differ. The sheet
belongs to the policy like an annex, is signed with it and gone through with it in the briefing.
1 Legal basis and chamber. Four fields: the profession’s duty of confidentiality (pre-filled with the provision
from section 1, with fields for version and reading date), the conduct rule on external providers (pre-filled for
the law firm with § 40(3) of the conduct rules, for the other two professions with “not searched — ask the
chamber”), the competent chamber, and the chamber’s publication on AI with date and status. Under each
field it says in italics where the pre-filled entry comes from and that it is to be read in the original.

SECTION 7
The chamber checklist and the entry into force
In brief: Twelve questions for all three professions and three to four per
profession — without answers, with fields for date and evidence; and the
plan with eighteen steps that begins with these questions.
A policy does not apply because it is written. The handbook derives from the supervisory authority two
requirements that are easily read as one: instructions are to be issued and documented. In the three
professions a third comes before them: before the instruction comes the question. Whoever instructs
their office clerk that client data may go into a particular tool has decided what professional law permits —
and can only do so if they asked beforehand. The chamber checklist is the evidence of the question; the
entry-into-force plan puts it at the start.
Source: Data Protection Conference (Germany), Orientierungshilfe of 6 May 2024, section 2.2, page 10 (read for Volume 2, retrieved 8
September 2026); Volume 2, section 9.
7.1 The chamber checklist
The file kammer-pruefliste-en.docx contains questions, not answers. It shows that the business asked
them — whom, when, with what result and what evidence. The questions come from the research for this kit:
they are the places where the search found a provision or a chamber publication but could not read it, and
the places where it found nothing. The list is not exhaustive, and the chamber may raise further questions;
the sheet says so in its header.
The twelve questions for all three professions:
1. Which provision establishes our confidentiality, in which version?
2. Has our chamber published a recommendation, guide or binding rule on the use of AI?
3. Is the entry of data under professional secrecy into an AI tool of a third-party provider a disclosure in the
chamber’s view — and under which conditions not?
4. Does a data processing agreement suffice, or does professional law require more?
5. Must clients or patients be informed about the use or consent to it?
6. How are auxiliary staff, trainees, interns, cleaners and external IT to be bound to confidentiality — is there
a chamber template?
7. Does an anonymised input or one prepared with placeholders count as permitted — and what counts as
anonymised?
8. Does the use affect our professional liability insurance?
9. How is the use to be documented?
10. Which body is competent for data protection questions, and has it made a statement on our profession?
11. Does the AI Act change anything about our professional duties?
12. What applies to tools built into our practice software?

Questions 3 and 4 are the two without which no approval for data under professional secrecy is possible;
question 5 decides point 10 of the labelling checklist; question 6 the column for the confidentiality
declaration; question 7 the italic sentence on the page “What never goes in here”. Every question names in
the last column where in the kit its answer lands. And every one has the same three fields: asked whom and
when, answer in one sentence, evidence.
The profession-specific questions. For the law firm: whether § 40(3) of the conduct rules in the bar’s view
also applies to AI tools; whether the bar’s checklist for AI service providers exists and whether it is to be
presented to the provider; which rule applies to escrow and third-party funds and to classified material. For
the tax advisory firm: which paragraphs of § 80 WTBG 2017 cover employees and whether the chamber’s
template suffices; whether login data for official portals and powers of representation are subject to their
own rules; how payroll and health data from payroll accounting are to be treated; whether there is a conduct
rule on external providers. For the medical practice: whether an AI provider can be “another person subject to
an obligation of secrecy” within the meaning of Art. 9(3) of the General Data Protection Regulation; how AI-
generated images in findings are to be treated; whether dictations may run through a third-party tool and
how that fits the documentation rules; which rules the practice-software provider and the electronic health

7.3 The follow-up — with the fifth trigger
The second sheet has a fixed date and five triggers. Four come from the handbook: a new or changed tool —

When every one of the eighteen rows carries a date in “done on” or is ticked “not applicable” with a reason,

business. In the three professions is added what this edition achieves and what it does not: it shows that the
business asked before it decided. Whether the answer was right it does not show — only the body that gave it
can.

Auxiliary persons (Hilfspersonen)
The word of the Physicians' Act; here all persons who have access to data under professional secrecy in a
firm or practice without being licensed professionals themselves: trainee lawyers, trainee accountants,
office staff, practice assistants, apprentices, interns, cleaners, external IT. Whom the profession’s duty of
confidentiality covers, the provision says; the supplement sheet records whom the business counts in.
Chamber (Kammer)
The statutory professional body responsible for the business: the regional bar association and the Austrian
Bar (Rechtsanwaltskammer, Rechtsanwaltskammertag), the Chamber of Tax Advisors and Auditors (Kammer
der Steuerberater:innen und Wirtschaftsprüfer:innen), the medical chamber (Ärztekammer). In the files, the
place where the business puts the questions of the chamber checklist; where it gives no information, a
professional takes its place.
Chamber checklist (Kammer-Prüfliste)
The file with twelve questions for all three professions and three to four per profession that are to be clarified
with the chamber or a professional before use; with fields for “asked whom”, date, answer and evidence,
without a given answer.
Clarification (Klärung)
The entry in the supplement sheet (section 5) and in the register (column “clarification with chamber /
professional”) that shows that a question of the chamber checklist was asked: whom, when, with what result
and evidence. Without date and evidence the clarification is not evidence.
Client data, patient data (Mandantendaten, Patientendaten)
The types of data in the register that fall under professional secrecy: client data (law firm), tax and levy data
(tax advisory), patient and health data (medical practice). Health data are at the same time a special
category under Art. 9 of the General Data Protection Regulation (Volume 1, section 11).
Confidentiality, duty of confidentiality (Verschwiegenheit, Verschwiegenheitspflicht)
The duty of licensed professionals and their auxiliary persons to keep silent about the matters and secrets
entrusted to them; the profession’s provision determines scope and exceptions. The kit never says what
confidentiality permits.
Confidentiality declaration (Verschwiegenheitserklärung)
The written obligation of an auxiliary person to confidentiality; not a sheet of this kit (there are chamber
templates), but its date stands in the overview per person of the training record.
Conduct rule, binding professional rule (Standesregel)
A rule issued by the chamber and binding on members of the profession (for instance the rules of
professional conduct for lawyers); as distinct from a recommendation or guide of the chamber. Whether a
publication is the one or the other, the supplement sheet records as a gap.
Licensed professional (Berufsträgerin, Berufsträger)
The person who carries confidentiality under professional law: lawyer (Rechtsanwältin/Rechtsanwalt), tax
advisor (Steuerberaterin/Steuerberater, licensed under the Public Accountants' Act), physician
(Ärztin/Arzt). In the files this person stands where the general kit says “management”.
Never-goes-in page (Nie-hinein-Seite)
The page “What never goes in here” per profession: rule for doubtful cases, list of the profession’s types of
data with tick boxes, the business’s rule, access, checking, reporting channel. Replaces in this edition the
one-page summary for posting of the general kit.
Professional secrecy (Berufsgeheimnis)
What a profession’s duty of confidentiality protects: the entrusted matters and secrets of clients or patients.
The files say “data under professional secrecy” for everything the profession’s duty of confidentiality covers

Rows 8 to 10, K1 to K5 (Zeilen 8 bis 10, K1 bis K5)
