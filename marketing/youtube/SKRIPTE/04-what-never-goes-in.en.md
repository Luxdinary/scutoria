### 1 Hook
[SCREEN: A1; Bildschirmtext ab Sekunde 0: „Synthetic voice · Sources on screen · No legal advice“]
Is someone in your office about to paste a customer complaint into an AI tool?
Five kinds of data that never go in, and one rule for everything else.

### 2 Problem
[SCREEN: A11, Handbuch Abschnitt 0, Absatz „The example business“, langsam scrollen]
We use one fictional business in every video: an electrical contractor with ten to forty-nine staff in Austria. Nobody in it is real. Three people in the office use a text assistant for quotes, summaries and translations. Two field staff set the same assistant up privately on their phones. Nobody decided any of this. It happened, one task at a time.

[SCREEN: Tool-Seite /tools, beide Karten sichtbar, ruhig]
Now look at what goes in on an ordinary day. A complaint letter to a customer, with the name, the address and what went wrong on site. A calculation sheet for a job, because the assistant is good at tidying up tables. The access code to a customer's building, because it sat in the same email as the job description. A colleague's sick note, to shorten it for the file. None of this was meant badly. Each person decided alone, in the moment, with nothing to lean on.

Three things follow.
[MARKER: Fact]
First: details that identify a customer, an employee or an applicant are personal data. Entering them into an AI application, and transferring them to the provider, each need a legal basis under the GDPR.
[SOURCE: Datenschutzkonferenz, Orientierungshilfe of 6 May 2024, sections 1.9 and 3.1, pp. 7 and 12 · retrieved 2026-10-23]
[MARKER: Fact]
Second: calculations, prices, contracts and customer lists are trade secrets only while the business takes reasonable steps to keep them secret.
[SOURCE: Directive (EU) 2016/943, Art. 2(1)(a) to (c) · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-18; § 26b(1)(3) UWG (Austria) · ris.bka.gv.at; § 2 no. 1(b) GeschGehG (Germany) · gesetze-im-internet.de · retrieved 2026-10-23]
Third: an access code in a tool is an access code out of your hands.

[MARKER: Interpretation]
Our interpretation: a rule moves that decision from each person at the keyboard to the business, once, in writing. That is what this video is about: the list, the rule of doubt, and two tools you can hand to your staff tomorrow.
<short pause>

### 3 Lösung
[SCREEN: Handbuch Abschnitt 2.3 „Prohibited inputs (§ 3)“, Absatz „What it rules“ halten]
Here is the list: section three of the usage policy in the AI Policy Toolkit, the same for every business. Five kinds of data never go into an AI tool, whatever the access.

One. Details about customers, staff and applicants by which a person can be identified: names, addresses, dates of birth, customer numbers, a licence plate.
Two. Access data: passwords, keys, access codes, tokens.
Three. Calculations, prices, contracts, customer lists and everything else the business keeps confidential.
Four. Health data: a sick note, a diagnosis, a word like "surgery" in an email about a colleague.
Five. Documents of third parties handed over in confidence: a customer's plans, a tender with a confidentiality clause.

[SCREEN: Handbuch 2.1, Aussage zur Datenschutzaufsicht, Quellenzeile zoomen]
[MARKER: Fact]
"Whatever the access" is the important part: the business account, a private account, the app on a private phone. The German data protection authorities advise that employees should not have to work through private accounts and devices, because profiles of individual employees can arise that way.
[SOURCE: Datenschutzkonferenz, Orientierungshilfe of 6 May 2024, section 2.4, pp. 10 to 11 · retrieved 2026-10-18]

The business adds its own kinds, in its own words, so that the people who handle them recognise them.

[SCREEN: Muster D2, oberer Rand: „In doubt: enter nothing, ask.“]
[MARKER: Interpretation]
Then comes the rule that closes the list. In doubt, enter nothing, and ask the named person. Our interpretation: a list without a rule of doubt is incomplete, because no catalogue meets every case. The rule only works if that person is reachable, in person or by phone. That is why it stands first on the one page, above the list.

[SCREEN: Handbuch 2.4 „Handling unavoidable personal data (§ 4)“]
Some tasks involve a person and cannot be split from it. The policy gives the business three ways to choose from. A task with personal data is not done with an AI tool at all. Or the text is drafted with placeholders and the details are inserted afterwards, outside the tool. Or the task is done only in a tool that the register approves for personal data.

[MARKER: Fact]
Why not just delete the name? Because that is regularly not enough. The data protection authorities state that removing names and addresses from an input often leaves the person identifiable, since the connection can be restored from the context, especially with applications whose purpose is to create cross-references from unstructured data.
[SOURCE: Datenschutzkonferenz, Orientierungshilfe of 6 May 2024, section 3.1, p. 12 · retrieved 2026-10-18]

[MARKER: Interpretation]
The placeholder rule is the practical middle way for a small business. You write the complaint with "customer" and "site" in square brackets instead of the details. Afterwards, outside the tool, somebody inserts them. The date of the visit, the street and the job can still point to a person, so the placeholders cover those too, not only the name.

[SCREEN: Handbuch 2.5 „Review before use (§ 5)“]
[MARKER: Fact]
One more habit belongs to the list: check before use. Figures, names, deadlines and references are read and checked by the person who uses the result, every time. The BSI, the German Federal Office for Information Security, asks that users can question the outputs of a model critically, adjust them where needed and restrict their reuse.
[SOURCE: BSI, Generative KI-Modelle, version 2.0 of 17 January 2025, M16, p. 49 · retrieved 2026-10-18]

[SCREEN: A13]
Two free tools turn this into something your staff can use today. Both run in your browser and send nothing anywhere. The first is called "May this go in?". Four questions: what is in the text or file, which account you would use, whether the provider trains its models on your inputs, and what the task is. You tick, and a traffic light answers. Red: no, not like this. Amber: not yet, clarify first. Green: yes, with the usual check. Under the light stand the reasons and what to do instead.

[MARKER: Fact]
The fourth question catches a case the list does not: a decision about a person. Hiring, assessment, promotion, termination, task allocation. AI tools are not used for that, whatever the data. Annex three of the AI Act lists systems for recruitment, selection, promotion, termination, task allocation and monitoring as high-risk.
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Annex III, point 4(a) and (b), consolidated version of 27 July 2026 · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[SCREEN: A14]
The second tool is called "Is there anything personal in it?". You paste a text. While you type, it marks email addresses, phone numbers, IBANs, names, addresses, health terms and access data. One click replaces them with placeholders. Its limit is stated on the page: it finds patterns. A name without a salutation, a nickname, a rare job title or an address without a house number can stay unmarked. Treat green as nothing obvious.
<short pause>

### 4 Beleg
[SCREEN: A3, dann A11]
Now the sources, with their markers.

[SCREEN: Handbuch Abschnitt 1, Aussagen zur Datenschutzkonferenz und zum BSI, Quellenzeilen zoomen]
[MARKER: Fact]
The list rests on two German bodies. The data protection authorities recommend clear, documented internal rules on whether, under which conditions and for which purposes which AI applications may be used.
[SOURCE: Datenschutzkonferenz, Orientierungshilfe Künstliche Intelligenz und Datenschutz, version 1.0 of 6 May 2024, sections 2.2, 2.4 and 3.1 · datenschutzkonferenz-online.de · retrieved 2026-10-18]
[MARKER: Fact]
The BSI asks employers to lay down which inputs are permitted and how outputs may be reused, bindingly, for example in policies.
[SOURCE: BSI, Generative KI-Modelle, Chancen und Risiken für Industrie und Behörden, version 2.0 of 17 January 2025, measure M16, p. 49 · bsi.bund.de · retrieved 2026-10-18]
[MARKER: Interpretation]
Our interpretation: both bodies are German. Their recommendations are professional practice, not law, and bind nobody outside Germany.

[SCREEN: Handbuch Abschnitt 1, Aussage zum Geschäftsgeheimnis, Quellenzeile zoomen]
[MARKER: Fact]
The strongest reason for the list is the trade secret. Under the Trade Secrets Directive, information is a trade secret only if it meets three conditions. The third: it has been subject to reasonable steps, under the circumstances, to keep it secret. Austria implements this in its Unfair Competition Act, Germany in its Trade Secrets Act.
[SOURCE: Directive (EU) 2016/943, Art. 2(1)(a) to (c) · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-18; § 26b(1)(3) UWG (Austria) · ris.bka.gv.at; § 2 no. 1(b) GeschGehG (Germany) · gesetze-im-internet.de · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[SCREEN: Handbuch Abschnitt 1, Unsicher-Kasten „Does an AI usage policy count as a reasonable step to keep information secret?“]
[MARKER: Uncertain]
This is open: whether a usage policy counts as such a reasonable step. We searched no court decision on this question. The handbook therefore says only this: without a rule, there is no evidence that the business took any step at all; with a rule, the business can show that it did. Whether that step is reasonable is decided case by case.
[SOURCE: Last checked 2026-10-19 · open point 8.12]

[SCREEN: Tool „May this go in?“, Quellenblock unten, Zeile zu Art. 9]
[MARKER: Fact]
Health data have their own rule. Article nine of the GDPR makes them a special category: processing is prohibited unless one of the exceptions in paragraph two applies.
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Art. 9(1) · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[SCREEN: Handbuch 2.8 „Reporting incidents (§ 8)“, Quellenzeile zoomen]
[MARKER: Fact]
And if it happens anyway: personal data that reach a tool or account the business has not approved can be a personal data breach. The controller notifies the supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, not later than seventy-two hours after becoming aware of it, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons. Every breach is documented.
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Art. 4(12), Art. 33(1) and (5) · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-23]
[MARKER: Interpretation]
Whether a given input is such a breach is decided case by case. That is why the policy says the report itself has no consequences for the person reporting: a business that punishes the report learns about incidents last.
<short pause>

### 5 Beispielbetrieb
[SCREEN: Muster D1, Seite mit § 3 und § 4, halten; Overlay „Example Electrical Ltd · fictional“]
Back to the fictional business, called Example Electrical Ltd in the sample documents. In the generator it answered question D1 and added two kinds of its own: the calculation sheets per job, and the access codes to customer sites. For unavoidable personal data it chose the placeholder rule: the complaint is written with "customer" and "site" in brackets, and the office manager inserts the details afterwards.
[MARKER: Interpretation]
The two field staff with the private app were the hardest field. Our interpretation: a rule that only names the tool silently permits its use through a private account. So the rule names tool and access path, and the business had to choose: either the two get access through a business account, or their work use on the private phone ends on the effective date.

[SCREEN: Muster D2 „AI at work: the one page“, ganze Seite, dann langsam von oben nach unten]
This is what comes out of it for the wall: the one page. At the top, the rule of doubt: "In doubt: enter nothing, ask." Then "This never goes into an AI tool, whatever the access": the five kinds and the two the business added, each with a box to tick during the briefing. Then "Only through the business access." Then "Check before use, every time." Then "It happened: report it", with the sentence that the report itself has no consequences.
[MARKER: Interpretation]
The page contains no section of the policy in full. Its content is taken from the policy and never decided separately, so that poster and policy cannot contradict each other.
<short pause>

### 6 Was es nicht ist
[SCREEN: A4]
What this is not. The sentence we use for the toolkit: it is not legal advice, and no document claims that your business complies with a rule. The documents show what you decided, since when, for which tools, and who knows about it.

The same goes for the two tools. The traffic light applies the nine areas of the usage policy; it is not legal advice and decides nothing on behalf of your business. The personal-data check finds patterns; treat green as nothing obvious.

[SCREEN: Handbuch Abschnitt 8, Zeile 8.12 halten]
What stays open. Whether a policy is a reasonable step to keep trade secrets: no court decision searched, open point eight twelve in the handbook.
[SOURCE: Last checked 2026-10-19 · open point 8.12]
[MARKER: Gap]
And the toolkit checked Austria and Germany only. For other member states it names the gap and points you to your own chamber or data protection authority. Your business decides what goes on its list and who is asked.
