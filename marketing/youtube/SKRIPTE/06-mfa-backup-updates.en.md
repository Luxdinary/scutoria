### 1 Hook
[SCREEN: A1; Bildschirmtext ab Sekunde 0 „Synthetic voice · Sources on screen · No legal advice“]
What do a security questionnaire from a customer and an application form from an insurer ask about first? The same things: multi-factor authentication, backups, updates.

### 2 Problem
[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 1 „Why you are asked“, Einleitung stehen lassen]
Requests of this kind reach a small business from a larger customer, from a contract with security clauses, or from a cyber insurer. Why the questionnaire comes is the subject of video two. Here we look at what the forms ask, and the insurers are the clearest case, because they write it down with numbers.

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 1.4 „Insurers“, langsam scrollen]
[MARKER: Fact]
We read six public documents of insurers in full: four application forms for cyber cover, one questionnaire for IT liability, one set of policy conditions. Most of them ask the same things: backups on separate systems with a tested restore; multi-factor authentication for cloud services, email, remote access and administrators; patches applied promptly and no software without manufacturer updates; protective software on all devices with automatic updates; a yearly training with anti-phishing; a written emergency plan; administrator rights only for administration. One of them, Beazley, calls four of these a mandatory condition for taking out cyber cover.
[SOURCE: Mannheimer, Fragebogen Cyber-Risiken (HF_060 0424); Markel, Pro Cyber v2; Allianz, IT-Haftpflicht 9.25; Beazley, Sicherheitsanforderungen; Funk with Beazley, July 2022; Donau, AVB Cyber 2025 · retrieved 2026-10-19 and 2026-10-23]

[MARKER: Interpretation]
The insurers ask largely what the law makes customers ask, in plainer words and with numbers. Our interpretation: for a business without an IT department, that is good news. The list is short, and one piece of evidence serves both.

[SCREEN: A10]
The typical situation: email has a second factor because the provider pushed for it; the cloud console does not. A backup runs every night to a disk in the office, and nobody has ever restored a file from it. One old computer in the workshop gets no updates. That is not negligence; it is what grows when IT is done on the side. The forms ask what is in place, what is partly there, and by when the rest will be.
<short pause>

### 3 Lösung
[SCREEN: A6, „What you get“ kurz halten, dann Handbuch online, Abschnitt 2.2]
Five measures, in the order the forms ask them, each with the piece of evidence that is enough.

One: multi-factor authentication on every email account.
[MARKER: Fact]
Beazley's critical requirement two and Funk's question two ask for MFA for cloud-based services, with cloud email as the example, and for all remote access. The Austrian label's question B six asks for MFA at least on critical systems. The NIS two Directive names multi-factor authentication in Article twenty-one, paragraph two, point j.
[SOURCE: Beazley, requirement 2; Funk, question 2; Cyber Trust Austria, Schema Policy 2026, B6; Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690, Annex 11.7.1; Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 21(2)(j); ENISA, 12 steps, step 5; Bundeskriminalamt, CEO Fraud, updated 29 June 2026 · retrieved 2026-10-19]
[MARKER: Interpretation]
That every mailbox counts as critical is our conclusion, not the label's wording: a captured mailbox is where payment fraud starts, and the first thing an attacker does with it is reset passwords elsewhere. The evidence: one dated screenshot of the MFA settings per system.

Two: MFA on remote access, on administrator accounts and on cloud consoles.
[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 2.2, Frage C3]
[MARKER: Fact]
Mannheimer asks whether administrators necessarily need MFA for internal servers and cloud structures, and whether home office access is possible only with MFA. Markel asks for MFA for all remote access and for administrators. Beazley: no remote access without VPN and MFA.
[SOURCE: Mannheimer, 7.7.1, 7.7.3, 8.1; Markel, section 4 point 6 and 6.2; Allianz, 2.5.5; Beazley, 2 and 3; Cyber Trust Austria, Schema Policy 2026, A6; Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690, Annex 11.3.2(a) and 6.7.2(d); Donau Versicherung, AVB Cyber 2025, Teil E · retrieved 2026-10-19]
[MARKER: Fact]
Which second factor? CISA ranks authenticator apps and security keys above codes by SMS; SMS comes last among the MFA methods, but still above a password alone.
[SOURCE: CISA, Implementing Phishing-Resistant MFA, October 2022 · retrieved 2026-10-19; NIST SP 800-63B-4, July 2025, section 3.2.5 · retrieved 2026-10-23]
[MARKER: Interpretation]
So: an app or a security key on email and administrator accounts first, SMS as the fallback. The evidence is the same screenshot, or the provider's administrator report.

Three: a daily backup with one copy out of an attacker's reach.
[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 2.3, Frage D1]
[MARKER: Fact]
Most insurer forms ask how often you back up and whether the media are separated from the active systems. Markel asks for backups on separated systems at least weekly below one million euro turnover, and daily above. Beazley's first critical requirement is tested offline backups. The Implementing Regulation under NIS two requires backup copies in a location that is not in the same network as the system. And the GDPR, which binds every business that handles personal data, requires the ability to restore personal data in a timely manner after an incident.
[SOURCE: Mannheimer, 4.1, 4.5, 4.6, 4.7, 4.9; Markel, section 4 point 5; Beazley, 1; Funk, 1; Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690, Annex 4.2.2(c); ENISA, 12 steps, step 9; Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 21(2)(c) · retrieved 2026-10-19; Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Art. 32(1)(a), (c), (d) · retrieved 2026-10-23]
[MARKER: Interpretation]
Out of reach is the whole point: a backup on a disk that is always connected, or in a cloud folder the office account can delete, is the next encrypted backup. Daily is the strictest rhythm in the forms, so the toolkit asks for daily. The evidence: a screenshot of the backup settings, and one sentence on where the copy out of reach is.

Four: a restore tested in the last twelve months, with the duration noted.
[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 2.3, Frage D2]
[MARKER: Fact]
Mannheimer asks for the date of the last successful restore test. Markel asks whether all backups are restore-tested at least once a year. The Implementing Regulation requires regular testing of the recovery of backup copies.
[SOURCE: Mannheimer, 4.2; Markel, 7.2; Beazley, 1; Funk, 1; Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690, Annex 4.2.6 · retrieved 2026-10-19; Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Art. 32(1)(a), (c), (d) · retrieved 2026-10-23]
[MARKER: Interpretation]
Noting the duration is our addition: it shows whether the IT is back within the time the business can bridge. One folder restored, the time stopped, date and result on one line. That line is the evidence.

Five: updates automatic within days, and protective software on every device.
[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 2.4, Fragen E1 und E3]
[MARKER: Fact]
The Austrian label's question B nine says no security update may be overdue for longer than one quarter. Markel asks whether security patches are installed within four weeks, and critical ones with a CVSS score of eight point zero or higher without delay.
[SOURCE: Cyber Trust Austria, Schema Policy 2026, B9; Markel, 7.3; Mannheimer, 7.1; Allianz, 2.5.3; Beazley; Funk, 5; Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690, Annex 6.6.1(a) to (c); ENISA, 12 steps, step 6 · retrieved 2026-10-19]
[MARKER: Fact]
On protection: Mannheimer asks whether all systems have protection against malware with automatic updates; Beazley and Funk ask for anti-virus on all devices and the scanning of incoming email.
[SOURCE: Mannheimer, 7.2; Markel, section 4 point 5 and 7.3; Beazley; Funk, 6 and 7; Cyber Trust Austria, Schema Policy 2026, B11 and B12; Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690, Annex 6.9.1 and 9; ENISA, 12 steps, step 6; Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 21(2)(h) · retrieved 2026-10-19; Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Art. 32(1)(a), (c), (d) · retrieved 2026-10-23]
[MARKER: Interpretation]
Within days is stricter than the label's quarter and Markel's four weeks; it is what automatic updates do on their own, and automatic is the only way a business without an IT department keeps up. Built-in protection answers every form we read. The evidence: a one-line note each month that the updates ran, and a device list with a column "supported until" and a column for encryption. Encryption matters: a lost laptop with personal data and without encryption is usually a breach you must report within seventy-two hours; with encryption, it is usually a lost laptop in the incident log.
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Art. 4(12), Art. 33(1) and (5), Art. 34(1) · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 3 „Evidence“, Absatz „How each piece of evidence arises in an hour or less“]
Five measures, five pieces of evidence. Where they exist, you collect them in an hour or less, as the handbook shows. What is not there yet gets a date, and the date goes into the document.

### 4 Beleg
[SCREEN: A3]
The markers: the square is a fact read at the original, with its retrieval date. The diamond is our interpretation, open to argument. The half circle: the sources contradict each other, or the law is open. The dashed circle: no source answers it, and your business decides.

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 2.2, Kasten „Which password rule should a supplier promise?“, auf den Kasten zoomen]
First, the password rule. This is open.
[MARKER: Uncertain]
The NIST digital identity guidelines of July 2025 require at least fifteen characters where the password is the only factor, and eight where a second factor is used. They forbid composition rules, and periodic change unless there is evidence of compromise. The German BSI argued publicly against forced changes on 31 January 2025 and again on 30 January 2026. The Implementing Regulation under NIS two points the other way: it requires the change of authentication data at predefined intervals.
[SOURCE: NIST SP 800-63B-4, July 2025, sections 3.1.1.1 and 3.1.1.2 · retrieved 2026-10-19; BSI press releases of 31 January 2025 and 30 January 2026 · retrieved 2026-10-19; Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690, Annex 11.6.2 · retrieved 2026-10-19]
The toolkit follows NIST and the BSI: long, unique, in a password manager, changed on suspicion, protected by a second factor. If a customer or an insurer insists on intervals and cites the Regulation, the honest answer is to agree an interval for the accounts without a second factor, and to keep MFA everywhere else. The self-declaration states which rule applies. ENISA's technical guidance on the Regulation reads it the toolkit's way: change passwords as necessary, for example after a security incident. Whether a court or an authority has ruled on the conflict, we have not searched.
[SOURCE: ENISA, NIS2 Technical Implementation Guidance, June 2025, p. 142, retrieved 2026-10-23]

[SCREEN: Memo „Where does our sign-in stand?“, Ergebnisseite, Befund mit gestricheltem Kreis]
Second, a gap.
[MARKER: Gap]
We found no source that sets a legal minimum for the sign-in security of a business outside NIS two. The standards above are what the authorities recommend and what questionnaires ask. A contract with a customer or an insurer may demand more.

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 1.4, Absatz mit der Raute]
Third, the rhythm.
[MARKER: Interpretation]
Where the forms differ, the toolkit takes the strictest: daily backup, a restore test once a year, updates within days. Our interpretation: the self-declaration has to hold in front of all of them. Markel's four weeks and Beazley's four mandatory conditions are the words of the forms, not of the law.
[SOURCE: Mannheimer, Fragebogen Cyber-Risiken (HF_060 0424); Markel, Pro Cyber v2; Allianz, IT-Haftpflicht 9.25; Beazley, Sicherheitsanforderungen; Funk with Beazley, July 2022; Donau, AVB Cyber 2025 · retrieved 2026-10-19 and 2026-10-23]

### 5 Beispielbetrieb

A password manager for the office but not for field staff: partly. An old workshop computer, no written device list: partly, second wave.

The five measures are the first questions of the forms, not a verdict on your business. The self-declaration has nine areas; these five sit in three of them.
