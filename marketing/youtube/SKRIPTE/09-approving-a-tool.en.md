### 2 Problem
[SCREEN: A9, Generator Toolkit 01, Block B, Frage B1 „Which kinds of AI tools are used in the business today?“ mit vorbelegtem Beispielbetrieb, dann auf B3 halten]
Here is how it goes in most small businesses. Tools arrive one by one. Somebody tried a text assistant for a quote, it worked, and now three people use it. Somebody else put a translation app on a private phone. Nobody said yes, and nobody said no. Nobody wrote down which tool, through which account, with which data.

The example in this video is a fictional electrical contractor with ten to forty-nine staff in Austria. No person in it is real. Three people in the office use a text assistant for quotes, summaries and translations. Two field staff set it up privately on their phones.
[SOURCE: AI Policy Toolkit, handbook, section 0, version of 23 October 2026 · scutoria.com]

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 1, die Aussage zum BSI mit Marker und Quellenzeile, auf die Quellenzeile zoomen]
[MARKER: Fact]
The German Federal Office for Information Security, the BSI, says in its measure M sixteen that employers should set which AI models and applications may be used for what, which inputs are permitted and how outputs are used further, bindingly, for example in policies.
[SOURCE: BSI, Generative KI-Modelle, Chancen und Risiken für Industrie und Behörden, version 2.0 of 17 January 2025, measure M16, p. 49 · bsi.bund.de · retrieved 2026-10-18]

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 1, die Aussage zur Datenschutzkonferenz mit Marker und Quellenzeile]
[MARKER: Fact]
The German data protection authorities recommend clear, documented internal rules on whether, under which conditions and for what which AI applications may be used.
[SOURCE: Datenschutzkonferenz, Orientierungshilfe Künstliche Intelligenz und Datenschutz, version 1.0 of 6 May 2024, sections 2.2, 2.4 and 3.1 · datenschutzkonferenz-online.de · retrieved 2026-10-18]

Both are guidance from Germany, not law, and the handbook marks them as such wherever it uses them. The question they leave with you is practical. On what basis do you say yes to a tool, and how do you show later that you checked?
<short pause>

### 3 Lösung
[SCREEN: Muster D3 Tool-Register, XLSX, Blatt 1, Kopfzeile und die drei Zeilen des Beispielbetriebs, Spalte „Approval“ mit „unclear“; Bildschirmtext „Unclear is a finding, not a failure“]
Start with a list, not with a judgement. The toolkit calls it the tool register: one row per tool actually in use. Tool kind, name and provider, purpose, users, account kind, data kinds, approval, the date it was checked, and the date it is due again. A tool that is used but not checked stands in the register with the entry "unclear" and gets a review date. Unclear is a finding, not a failure. A row without a date is not evidence. Private accounts get "no", with the reason: section one of the policy.
[SOURCE: AI Policy Toolkit, handbook, section 3, version of 23 October 2026 · scutoria.com]

[SCREEN: Muster D3 Freigabeformular, PDF, Seite 1, Kopf und die Tabelle mit den sieben Fragen; Bildschirmtext „Seven questions per tool“]
Then the check, one tool at a time. The approval form asks seven questions per tool. Questions one to four are the contractual criteria. Five and six come from the guidance on accounts and default settings. Seven is a classification a business usually cannot make itself.

[SCREEN: Freigabeformular, Zoom auf Frage 1 bis 4]
Question one. Training with business data. Are your inputs used to train the provider's models, and can this be excluded or opted out?

Question two. Data processing agreement. Is one offered, and for which services?

Question three. Place of processing. Is there a reliable commitment to processing in the Union, and with which exceptions?

Question four. Third-party rights. Does the provider indemnify you against claims by third parties, and what is excluded? Typically your own inputs are.

[SCREEN: Freigabeformular, Zoom auf Frage 5 bis 7]
Question five. Deletion and input history. Can it be set that no input history is kept beyond the session, and that inputs are not used for training?

Question six. Access and account kind. Is there a business account without personal names, with a functional address, and who manages it?

Question seven. Lawfulness of model development. Is there an official or court finding about the provider? A business without a legal department usually leaves this line as "not surveyed". That is more honest than a tick.
[SOURCE: AI Policy Toolkit, handbook, section 3, version of 23 October 2026 · scutoria.com]

[SCREEN: Freigabeformular, die Spalten „Answer“, „Source at the provider, date“ und „Wording / remark“; Raute Interpretation]
[MARKER: Interpretation]
Our interpretation, and the rule the form follows: each question is checked at the provider's own page or contract, not at an article about the provider. The concluded contract prevails over a product page. Next to each answer you note where you read it and on which date. A blank field is a finding: "no statement on the page checked". A filled field without source and date is not a finding. This video names no provider, and the toolkit ships no filled approval list, because a list would be wrong the moment a provider changes its terms.

[SCREEN: A9, Frage I4 „What is the standard for approving a tool?“ mit den drei Antworten, Vorbelegung sichtbar]
Before the first form is filled, the management sets one standard for every tool alike. The generator asks this in question I four and offers three answers. Without a data processing agreement, no approval for personal data. Without an opt-out from training on your inputs, no approval at all. Or: decided per tool. The toolkit recommends none of the three, because the right one depends on what the business does with the tool. The example business chose the first. The generator shows that choice as its prefilled value, not as a recommendation.
[SOURCE: AI Policy Toolkit, handbook, section 3, version of 23 October 2026 · scutoria.com]

[SCREEN: Muster D6 Plan der Inkraftsetzung, XLSX, Schritte 2, 6 und 7 im Bild]
In the enactment plan, three of the fifteen steps carry this work. Step two: take stock of which tools are actually in use and create the register, also with "unclear". Step six: set up business accounts, set the options for training and input history, use a functional address. Step seven: the approval check per tool, fill in the form, update the register.
[SOURCE: AI Policy Toolkit, handbook, section 6, version of 23 October 2026 · scutoria.com]

### 4 Beleg
[SCREEN: A11, Handbuch online, Abschnitt 3, die Aussage zu Artikel 28 mit Marker und Quellenzeile, Zoom auf die Quellenzeile]
What stands behind the questions.

[MARKER: Fact]
Question two is the GDPR. Where a provider processes personal data on behalf of your business, Article twenty-eight, paragraph three, of the GDPR requires a contract or other legal act that binds the processor. That contract is the data processing agreement.
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Art. 28(3) · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 3, die Aussage zur Orientierungshilfe 2.5 mit Quellenzeile]
[MARKER: Fact]
Questions five and six come from the German data protection authorities. As an example of data protection by design and by default under Article twenty-five of the GDPR, they name accounts for employees whose functions for training and input history are set when the account is put into operation, so that no inputs are processed for training and no input history is kept beyond the session. Outputs belonging to the account must not be published automatically.
[SOURCE: Datenschutzkonferenz, Orientierungshilfe of 6 May 2024, section 2.5, p. 11; on input history also section 1.10, p. 7 · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 2.1, die Aussage zur Orientierungshilfe 2.4 mit Quellenzeile, darunter die Raute Interpretation]
[MARKER: Fact]
The same authorities advise that employees should not have to work with AI applications through private accounts and devices, because profiles of individual employees can arise that way. Accounts should not contain the names of individual employees where the application does not run on the business's own servers, and a functional email address of the business is called for where an address is requested.
[SOURCE: Datenschutzkonferenz, Orientierungshilfe of 6 May 2024, section 2.4, pp. 10 to 11 · retrieved 2026-10-18]

[MARKER: Interpretation]
Our interpretation: a rule that only names the tool silently permits its use through a private account. The rule must therefore name the tool and the access path. That is why the register has a column for the account kind: business, shared, private or unknown.

Questions one, three and four are contractual criteria: the answers stand in the provider's terms and change when the terms change.

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 1, die Auslegung zur schriftlichen Richtlinie mit Quellenzeile; Raute Interpretation]
[MARKER: Interpretation]
Does any law require a written policy or a register? Our interpretation: neither the AI Act nor the GDPR expressly requires a written AI policy. Article four of the AI Act, Article twenty-four, paragraph one, of the GDPR and the trade secrets directive come closest. The register is the part of the policy that names the tools.
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Art. 4; Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Art. 24(1); Directive (EU) 2016/943, Art. 2(1)(c) · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-18 and 2026-10-19]

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 6, die Aussage zu Artikel 24 Absatz 1 und die Lücke darunter; Quadrat Fact, dann gestrichelter Kreis Gap]
[MARKER: Fact]
Where personal data are involved, the review is in the regulation itself. The controller's technical and organisational measures "shall be reviewed and updated where necessary".
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Art. 24(1) · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[MARKER: Gap]
No source names an interval. The toolkit offers six or twelve months and recommends neither. A tool is checked again on its review date, and whenever the provider changes its terms, whichever comes first.

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 6, der Kasten zu Schritt 5; halber Kreis Uncertain]
[MARKER: Uncertain]
This is open: whether a new AI tool triggers the participation of a works council is national labour law and differs between member states. Article eighty-eight, paragraph one, of the GDPR allows member states to lay down more specific rules on employee data. The German data protection authorities write that introducing an AI application will in some cases trigger co-determination. A business with a works council clarifies this with a specialist before it procures a tool, not after.
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Art. 88(1) · retrieved 2026-10-23; Datenschutzkonferenz, Orientierungshilfe of 6 May 2024, section 1.12, p. 8 · retrieved 2026-10-18; Datenschutzkonferenz, Orientierungshilfe of 6 May 2024, section 2.2, p. 10 · retrieved 2026-10-23]

### 5 Beispielbetrieb
[SCREEN: Muster D3 Tool-Register, XLSX, die drei Zeilen des Beispielbetriebs; Bildschirmtext „Example Electrical Ltd · fictional“]
Back to the fictional electrical contractor. Its answers in block B of the generator become three rows in the register. Row one: a text assistant, used in the office for drafts of quotes, summaries and translations, through a business account. Approval: unclear, with a review date. Row two: a translation service. Account kind: unknown, with the note: clarify first, then check. Row three: the assistant inside the office software, through a shared account, with the note that a shared account carries no personal names and uses a functional address. The office management keeps the register; that was question A five.
[SOURCE: AI Policy Toolkit, handbook, sections 0 and 3, version of 23 October 2026 · scutoria.com]

[SCREEN: A9, Frage B3 und B4 mit den vorbelegten Antworten „Yes“ und „Not permitted“]
The two field staff with the private phones are the reason the policy cannot say "as before". Question B three was answered yes: tools are used through private accounts. Question B four sets the rule from now on: not permitted. The handbook says what that means for them. Either they get access through a business account, or their work use on the private phone ends on the effective date.

[SCREEN: Muster D3 Freigabeformular, PDF, Seite 1, langsam scrollen vom Kopf bis zu den Unterschriftszeilen]
This is the approval form the generator produces for the text assistant. One page per tool. The head is prefilled: the tool, its kind, the purpose, the account kind, the checking person, office management. The seven questions follow, each with a field for the answer, a field for the source at the provider with its date, and a field for the wording. The generator leaves these fields empty on purpose, because it does not know the provider and should not guess. Below that: the decision, the reason, and the standard of the business, prefilled from question I four: without a data processing agreement, no approval for personal data. Then review due, and the two signature lines. The last line says what happens afterwards: update the row in the tool register and file this form with it.

[SCREEN: A15, Muster D7 Nachweisseite, Statuszeilen; Bildschirmtext „Listed, not hidden“]
On the evidence summary, D seven, every piece of evidence is one line with a status: regulated, planned or open. Open items are listed, not hidden.

### 6 Was es nicht ist
[SCREEN: A4, Fußzeile „Source-based. No legal advice.“]
What this toolkit is not, in the words of the product page: It is not legal advice, and no document claims that your business complies with a rule. The documents show what you decided, since when, for which tools, and who knows about it.

The approval form sets no benchmark; the management decides. A signed form shows that you checked, where, and when. It does not show that the provider's terms are enough for every task you have in mind, and it does not replace the question of what goes into the tool at all.

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 8, die Tabelle der offenen Punkte, langsam scrollen]
The handbook lists seventeen points, checked as of 23 October 2026; eight remain open. Two touch this topic. Whether a policy binds freelancers and contractors is contract law and was not surveyed. Whether an AI tool triggers co-determination is national law, and the plan points to a specialist. Your business decides, and the documents record the decision with a date.
[SOURCE: AI Policy Toolkit, handbook, section 8, version of 23 October 2026 · scutoria.com]
