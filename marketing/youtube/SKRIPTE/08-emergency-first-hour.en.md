### 2 Problem
[SCREEN: A6, Produktseite Toolkit 02, auf der Zeile D3 „Emergency card“ anhalten]
The toolkit's question about past incidents names three examples: ransomware, a hacked mailbox, a fraudulent payment. Each starts the same way: somebody notices something, and for a while nobody knows how much is affected.

In that first hour, three things come together. First, the people who could help are not in the room. The IT provider, a hotline, the police, the insurer: their numbers are in the address book of the computer that is encrypted, or in the mailbox that somebody else is now using. Second, decisions are made under pressure. Switch the machine off or leave it running. Reinstall or wait. Pay or not pay. Some of these decisions destroy the traces that the police and the insurer will later ask for. Third, the clocks start before anyone has thought about clocks.

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 2.5, Frage F2, Quellenzeile zoomen]
[MARKER: Fact]
The data protection deadline runs from the moment the business becomes aware of a breach.
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Art. 4(12), Art. 33(1) and (5), Art. 34(1) · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-23]
The insurer's conditions name a deadline of their own, and a customer covered by NIS two has a clock of its own, which your call may start.
[SOURCE: Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690, Annex 5.1.4(d); Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 23(1) and (4); NISG 2026, § 34(1) and (2); BSI-Gesetz, § 32(1); Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, Art. 30(2)(f); Donau Versicherung, AVB Cyber 2025, Teil D 7.1.4 · retrieved 2026-10-23]

A business without an IT department has nobody whose job this is. The fictional example business of the toolkit, an electrical contractor in Austria, shows the usual picture: no emergency plan, nobody named for the data protection question, no incident log. The card in this video answers exactly these three things: whom to call, what to do, which clock runs. On paper.

### 3 Lösung
[SCREEN: A16, Muster D3 ganz, zwei Sekunden halten, dann Zoom auf „The first hour“]
The emergency card is document D3 of the Security Evidence Toolkit: one page for the wall and the drawer. Its first part is the first hour, in four steps.

[MARKER: Interpretation]
Our interpretation, drawn from three public sources that we name in a moment: disconnect; do not delete; call; write down.
[SOURCE: onlinesicherheit.gv.at, Erpressungstrojaner: Was tun bei Infektion?, last updated 23 July 2022 · retrieved 2026-10-23; BSI and partners, TOP 12 Maßnahmen bei Cyber-Angriffen, September 2019; BSI and Allianz für Cyber-Sicherheit, IT-Notfallkarte · retrieved 2026-10-19]

Step one, disconnect. Pull the network cable and switch Wi-Fi off. If it is unclear which device is affected, disconnect the router from the internet.

Step two, do not delete and do not reinstall. Stop the backup jobs and disconnect the NAS or the backup disk from the network. Never connect a backup to an infected device.

Step three, call. The responsible person, the IT provider, the hotline. The numbers are on the card, not in the computer.

Step four, write down. The time, the device, what was on the screen, who did what. A photo of the screen with the phone is enough; the authority and the insurer will ask for it.

[SCREEN: Zoom auf die Zeile zum Lösegeld auf der Karte]
[MARKER: Fact]
One more line on the card: no ransom. The Austrian government's security portal says that experts advise against paying.
[SOURCE: onlinesicherheit.gv.at, Erpressungstrojaner: Was tun bei Infektion?, last updated 23 July 2022 · retrieved 2026-10-23; BSI and partners, TOP 12 Maßnahmen bei Cyber-Angriffen, September 2019; BSI and Allianz für Cyber-Sicherheit, IT-Notfallkarte · retrieved 2026-10-19]
[MARKER: Interpretation]
Our interpretation: no payment without talking to the police and the insurer first, because a payment guarantees nothing.

[SCREEN: Zoom auf „Who to call“ der Karte, Fassung Österreich; Bildschirmtext „0800 888 133“]
Now, whom to call. The card takes the contacts from the country you name. For Austria, the numbers were read at the originals.
[MARKER: Fact]
The Cyber-Security-Hotline of the Austrian chambers of commerce answers at 0800 888 133, around the clock, free for members. CERT.at, the national computer emergency team, takes reports at plus 43 1 505 64 16 78 and by email. The police emergency number is 133, the European number is 112. The data protection authority takes breach notifications by online form, PDF form or email. Numbers change. The card carries the date on which they were checked and says: check them before printing.
[SOURCE: WKO, Cyber-Security-Hotline, 1 June 2026 · wko.at · retrieved 2026-10-23; CERT.at · retrieved 2026-10-19; polizei.gv.at, Notrufnummern · retrieved 2026-10-19; Datenschutzbehörde, Meldung Data Breach · retrieved 2026-10-19; onlinesicherheit.gv.at, Erpressungstrojaner, 23 July 2022 · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 5.2 Germany, Quellenzeile zoomen]
[MARKER: Fact]
For Germany, the card names the police emergency number 110 and the European 112. The police of each federal state runs a Zentrale Ansprechstelle Cybercrime for businesses, listed at polizei.de, and the data protection authority is the one of your federal state. The ZAC and the state authority you fill in yourself.
[SOURCE: BSI and Allianz für Cyber-Sicherheit, IT-Notfallkarte; BSI and partners, TOP 12 Maßnahmen bei Cyber-Angriffen, September 2019; polizei.de, ZAC; BfDI, Landesbehörden · retrieved 2026-10-19; Bundesnetzagentur, Notruf · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 5.3, Marker Lücke zoomen]
[MARKER: Gap]
For other member states, this is a gap: the toolkit did not survey their authorities, hotlines or labels. The card then names the European emergency number, the national computer emergency team, for which ENISA keeps a list, and the national data protection authority, and leaves the hotline open for you to fill in from your own chamber or authority.

[SCREEN: Zoom auf „Deadlines“ der Karte; dann Handbuch online, Abschnitt 4.2]
The second part of the card is the deadlines. Three clocks.

[MARKER: Fact]
Clock one, personal data. Article thirty-three of the GDPR: a controller notifies a personal data breach to the supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, not later than 72 hours after having become aware of it, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons. A late notification carries the reasons for the delay. Where the risk is high, the people concerned are informed as well. And every breach is documented, whether it is reported or not.
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Art. 33(1) to (3), Art. 34(1) and (3)(a), English and German text; Datenschutzbehörde, Meldung Data Breach; BfDI, Landesbehörden · retrieved 2026-10-23]
[MARKER: Interpretation]
Our interpretation: the 72 hours are the hardest deadline a small business faces. They run from awareness, over weekends, and the first decision, whether personal data is affected at all, needs a named person. That person and the person who reports stand on the card.

[MARKER: Fact]
Clock two, customers under NIS two. A covered entity sends an early warning within 24 hours of becoming aware of a significant incident, a notification within 72 hours, and a final report no later than one month after that notification.
[SOURCE: Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690, Annex 5.1.4(d); Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 23(1) and (4); NISG 2026, § 34(1) and (2); BSI-Gesetz, § 32(1); Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, Art. 30(2)(f); Donau Versicherung, AVB Cyber 2025, Teil D 7.1.4 · retrieved 2026-10-23]
[MARKER: Interpretation]
Our interpretation: if your incident is significant for your customer, your call may be the moment its 24 hours start. That is why the card shows the deadline you promised to customers. If your own business is covered by NIS two, the same clocks apply to you, towards your CSIRT in Austria or the BSI in Germany. The free NIS two memo on scutoria.com says whether it is.
[SOURCE: Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 23(4); Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Art. 33(1) · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[MARKER: Fact]
Clock three, the insurer and the bank. The policy names its own deadline. The Austrian conditions we read require notice without undue delay, at the latest within one week, and a call to the insurer's hotline without undue delay, so that experts can be engaged: call first, then write. After a fraudulent transfer, the criminal police office says: contact your bank immediately, request a recall, preserve the emails and the screenshots, and report at a police station.
[SOURCE: Donau Versicherung, AVB Cyber 2025, Teil D 7.1.4 · retrieved 2026-10-23; Bundeskriminalamt, CEO Fraud, updated 29 June 2026 · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[SCREEN: Zoom auf „Afterwards“ und „Where things are“ der Karte]
The last part of the card is for afterwards.
[MARKER: Fact]
The GDPR requires the breach to be documented with its facts, effects and remedial action. The TOP twelve include checking access rights: new passwords, second factor.
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Art. 33(5); Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690, Annex 3.6; BSI, TOP 12 · retrieved 2026-10-19]
[MARKER: Interpretation]
Our interpretation, as it stands on the card: from a clean device, change the passwords of email, bank and cloud, check the second factor, and write one page: what happened, how, what was missing, what was changed. That page is the incident log, and it is what makes the next self-declaration true.
<short pause>

### 4 Beleg
[SCREEN: A11, Handbuch online, Abschnitt 4.1, Marker und Quellenzeile zoomen]
Where does this come from, and what is open. Three public sources stand behind the first hour.

[MARKER: Fact]
The Austrian government's security portal advises that an infected computer should be cut off from power immediately, that experts advise against paying the ransom, and that the incident should be reported at the nearest police station. The TOP twelve measures of the BSI and its partners ask whether affected systems and the internet connection were disconnected, whether backups were stopped and protected, whether logs, notes and photos of screens were preserved, whether police or authorities were informed after coordination, and whether access rights were checked. The BSI's IT-Notfallkarte, modelled on the fire notice, carries three messages: know whom to call and how to reach them, pass on the decisive information at once, take countermeasures only after consultation.
[SOURCE: onlinesicherheit.gv.at, Erpressungstrojaner: Was tun bei Infektion?, last updated 23 July 2022 · retrieved 2026-10-23; BSI and partners, TOP 12 Maßnahmen bei Cyber-Angriffen, September 2019; BSI and Allianz für Cyber-Sicherheit, IT-Notfallkarte · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[SCREEN: A3, die vier Marker-Kacheln, Raute zoomen]
[MARKER: Interpretation]
The four steps are our combination of these three sources. No source lists them in this order, and the footnote of the card says so.

[SCREEN: halber Kreis zoomen; dann Handbuch 4.1, zweiter Absatz]
[MARKER: Uncertain]
One point is open, and the card names it. On switching off, the sources differ. The Austrian portal says cut the power. The TOP twelve want traces preserved, and a hard power cut destroys some of them. The card follows our weighing: do not switch off if help arrives shortly, because traces are lost; switch off if nobody comes and the encryption spreads visibly. This is open, and you may weigh it differently with your IT provider.

[SCREEN: gestrichelter Kreis zoomen]
[MARKER: Gap]
And one gap, named a moment ago: the contacts of other member states. The card leaves those fields open on purpose.

What is not in this video: no statistics and no case. The handbook cites no figures on how often this happens, and neither do we.

### 6 Was es nicht ist
[SCREEN: Produktseite Toolkit 02, Absatz „What it is not“, halten; dann Handbuch online, Abschnitt 6, Zeilen 6.22, 6.23]
What the card is not. It is not legal advice, not an audit and not a certificate, and no document claims that your business is secure or complies with a law. The documents show what you have in place on a given date, what you will do by when, and who answers for it.

The handbook lists its open points, and three of them touch this topic. Whether a court has ruled on "without undue delay" for small suppliers was not searched. The German court decisions on cyber insurance applications are known from secondary sources only. The contacts of other member states were not surveyed. And the question of switching off is a weighing, not a rule. Your business decides, with its IT provider and, if it has one, its insurer.
