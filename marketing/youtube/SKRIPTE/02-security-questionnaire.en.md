### 2 Problem
[SCREEN: A6, Produktseite Toolkit 02, Kopf mit Lead-Text, dann langsam nach unten]
The situation is the same in many small businesses. A larger customer sends a document with questions about information security: multi-factor authentication, backups, updates, incidents, training, your own providers. There is a date by which it is due. There is no IT department. The office manager opens it, the external IT provider is out on a job, and the questions use words nobody in the business uses every day.

[SCREEN: A10, Generator Toolkit 02, eine Statusfrage mit den vier Antworten, Cursor ruht]
Two things usually happen next. The first: the questionnaire goes to the IT provider, who answers the technical questions and leaves the rest blank. Who is responsible, where the emergency plan is, whether staff have been briefed: those are not technical questions, and nobody answers them. The second: someone ticks "yes" everywhere, because "yes" seems to be what the customer wants.

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 0, Absatz „The four answers“, Zoom auf den Satz mit dem „yes“]
The handbook of our toolkit puts it this way: a questionnaire that gets an honest "not yet" learns more than from a "yes" that falls apart at the first follow-up question. A customer who asks for your last restore test record and gets none has learned something about every other "yes" on the form.
[SOURCE: Security Evidence Toolkit, handbook of 23 October 2026, section 0 · scutoria.com]

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 1, Einleitung mit den vier Arten von Anfragen]
Four kinds of requests arrive at small businesses: a questionnaire from a larger customer, a contract with security clauses, the application form of a cyber insurer, and the wish to show a label or a certificate. This video is about the first, and about the contract that often follows it.
[SOURCE: Security Evidence Toolkit, handbook of 23 October 2026, section 1 · scutoria.com]

### 3 Lösung
[SCREEN: A6, Produktseite, die drei Schritte „Answer, Download, Send“]
What the business does, in five steps. All five work without any toolkit; ours only makes them faster.

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 1.1, Absatz zu Artikel 21, Quellenzeile zoomen]
[MARKER: Fact]
Step one: understand who is asking and why. A customer covered by the NIS two Directive must take appropriate and proportionate measures in at least ten areas, and one of them is supply chain security, including the relationship with its direct suppliers. When it decides which measures are appropriate, it must take into account the cybersecurity practices of those suppliers.
[SOURCE: Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 21(1), (2)(d) and (3) · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[MARKER: Interpretation]
Our interpretation: that is the questionnaire. The Directive does not tell the customer what to ask you; it obliges the customer to consider your practices, and a questionnaire is the cheapest way to do that for hundreds of suppliers at once. So the questions follow the customer's own ten areas, and your answers can follow them too. The Directive binds the customer, not you. What binds you is the contract that follows, and, wherever you handle personal data, the GDPR itself.

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 1.1, Absatz zu Artikel 32 DSGVO]
[MARKER: Fact]
Article thirty-two of the GDPR requires every controller and processor to take appropriate technical and organisational measures, among them the ability to restore personal data in a timely manner after an incident, and a process for regularly testing the measures.
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Art. 32(1)(a) to (d) · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[SCREEN: A10, die Statusfrage, Cursor wandert zu „not yet with a date“]
Step two: answer every question with one of four answers. In place. Partly. Not yet, with a date. Not applicable, with a reason. Nothing else. Not "yes" when it is "partly", and not a blank when it is "not yet". An answer like "not yet, by the end of the quarter, responsible: the office manager with the IT provider" is complete. It names a gap, a date and a person.
[SOURCE: Security Evidence Toolkit, handbook of 23 October 2026, section 0 · scutoria.com]

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 1.6, Quellenzeile zoomen]
[MARKER: Fact]
Step three: measure by your size. The Directive ties the measures to the size of the entity and to its exposure to risk. The Implementing Regulation repeats that and lets an entity document why a requirement is not appropriate for it.
[SOURCE: Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 21(1) second subparagraph; Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690, Art. 2(2) second subparagraph and recital 5; NISG 2026, § 32(3); NISV 2026, § 4 · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[MARKER: Interpretation]
Our interpretation: proportionality is written for your customer, not for its suppliers, and no law stops a customer from asking more of you than of itself. But a customer that must justify its own measures by its size has a good reason to accept your answers measured by your size, and you may say so, in the first paragraph of your cover letter. Nothing in our toolkit asks a small business for a security operations centre, penetration tests or a certification.

[SCREEN: Muster D4 Nachweisliste (example-electrical-ltd-evidence-list-en.pdf), Spalten Frage, Beleg, Anlage, Datum]
Step four: evidence. An on-site audit of a ten-person supplier is rare. What the reviewer wants is a dated document per answer that could be shown if someone asked. Most of these exist already or take an hour or less. The account list with daily and administrator accounts: one table. Screenshots of the MFA settings: one per system. The written rules: two pages, signed. The backup settings and the place of the copy out of reach: one screenshot and one sentence. The restore test: one line with date, duration and result. The emergency card: printed, dated, posted. The update check: one line per month. The incident log: one page, started. The attendance list of the briefing: date, topics, names. What you cannot show on the day goes into the evidence list with the date from your action plan, and the cover letter says so.
[SOURCE: Security Evidence Toolkit, handbook of 23 October 2026, section 3 · scutoria.com]

[SCREEN: Muster D5 Antwortschreiben (example-electrical-ltd-cover-letter-en.pdf), erster Absatz, dann Abgleichstabelle]
Step five: send it as a self-declaration, not as a filled-in form. The answers with their status and date, signed by the management. The evidence, numbered as attachments. And a cover letter that names the gaps with their dates in plain words and maps the customer's questions to your sections. Then freeze a dated copy in your evidence folder and note when you will review it.
<short pause>

### 4 Beleg
[SCREEN: A11, Handbuch online, Abschnitt 1.7, Überschrift, Marker und Quellenzeile]
Now the sources behind the core claim of this video: that "not yet, with a date" is a complete answer.

[MARKER: Fact]
Fact. The NIS two Directive obliges an entity that finds it does not comply with the measures to take, without undue delay, all necessary, appropriate and proportionate corrective measures. Austria's NIS Act of 2026 expects an audit report with the identified deficiencies and an action plan to remedy them.
[SOURCE: Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 21(4); Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690, Annex 2.2.1; NISG 2026, § 33(3) · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 1.7, zweiter Fakt, Quellenzeile zu § 45 zoomen]
[MARKER: Fact]
Fact. Austria makes the point in its penalty provisions. Failing to implement the measures is an offence only insofar as the authority did not learn of it solely from the entity's own self-declaration. Knowingly false statements in a self-declaration are an offence of their own.
[SOURCE: NISG 2026, § 45(1) point 3 and (4) point 8 · ris.bka.gv.at · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[MARKER: Interpretation]
Our interpretation: the law treats a documented gap with a plan as a normal state. Austria even spares the gap that an entity reports itself, and punishes the false answer instead. A supplier that writes "not yet, planned by the end of the quarter, responsible: the office manager with the IT provider" gives the customer what the customer must produce for itself. That is the whole reasoning behind the four answers and the action plan.

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 1.1, Absatz zum NISG § 33]
[MARKER: Fact]
Fact, for Austria. The Austrian act requires essential and important entities to send the authority a structured self-declaration within twelve months of the registration duty arising, in particular on the security of their supply chains.
[SOURCE: BSI-Gesetz of 2 December 2025, BGBl. 2025 I No. 301, last amended on 23 July 2026, BGBl. 2026 I No. 226 · gesetze-im-internet.de · retrieved 2026-10-23; NISG 2026, BGBl. I No. 94/2025 of 23 December 2025, § 32(4), § 33(1) · ris.bka.gv.at · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[MARKER: Interpretation]
Our interpretation: for an Austrian customer, your questionnaire is the raw material of its own self-declaration. Whoever asks you in 2026 is collecting what it has to send to the authority in 2027. That explains the tone and the deadlines of the requests; it does not make your answers a filing with the authority.

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 1.2, die acht Vertragsinhalte, Quellenzeile zoomen]
[MARKER: Fact]
Fact, on the contract that follows the questionnaire. For digital providers across the Union, the Commission's Implementing Regulation of 17 October 2024 says what goes into their contracts with suppliers: cybersecurity requirements; training and, where appropriate, certifications of the supplier's staff; background checks; an obligation to report incidents without undue delay; the right to audit or to receive audit reports; an obligation to handle vulnerabilities; requirements on subcontracting; and obligations at the end of the contract. Eight contents.
[SOURCE: Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690 of 17 October 2024, Annex points 5.1.4 and 5.2 · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[MARKER: Fact]
In Austria, the regulation of the cybersecurity authority of 1 October 2026 applies the same Annex to every essential and important entity.
[SOURCE: NISV 2026, BGBl. II No. 290/2026 of 1 October 2026, § 2 · ris.bka.gv.at · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[MARKER: Interpretation]
Our interpretation: those eight contents are the pattern of the clauses you will see, from digital providers anywhere in the Union and from every NIS two customer in Austria. A German customer outside the digital sectors is not bound to the Annex by law, but its auditors and lawyers read the same text.

[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 6, Zeilen 6.6 und 6.22, Marker Gap]
[MARKER: Gap]
And what is open. We looked for an official model supplier questionnaire and found none; the questions in our toolkit follow the sources named at each question. Whether a court has ruled on what "without undue delay" means for a small supplier: not searched. Both points stand in section six of the handbook.
[SOURCE: Security Evidence Toolkit, handbook of 23 October 2026, section 6, points 6.6 and 6.22 · scutoria.com]
