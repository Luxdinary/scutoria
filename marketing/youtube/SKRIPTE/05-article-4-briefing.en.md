## Script

### 1 Hook
[SCREEN: A1, Startseite scutoria.com, Claim in drei Schlägen; ab Sekunde 0 Bildschirmtext „Synthetic voice · Sources on screen · No legal advice“]
Does the AI Act require you to train your staff, and to prove it?


Here: what Article four asks, what the Commission accepts as proof, and the record you keep.
<short pause>

### 2 Problem
[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 0, Absatz „The example business“; Bildschirmtext „Example Electrical Ltd · fictional“]
Take the business we use in every example. It is invented: an electrical contractor in Austria with ten to forty-nine staff. Three people in the office use a text assistant for quotes, summaries and translations. Two field staff have set it up privately on their phones. Nobody has been briefed, and nothing is written down.

Then the owner hears three things. The AI Act requires training. Course offers arrive, with a certificate at the end. And somebody mentions fines. Three questions follow. What exactly must we do? Must we prove it? And how?

[MARKER: Fact]
The duty exists. It is Article four of the AI Act, and it has applied since 2 February 2025.
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Art. 4 and Art. 113, point (a), consolidated version of 27 July 2026; Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, Art. 1, point (5) · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[MARKER: Interpretation]
But the duty is smaller than the headline, and the proof is simpler than the course offer. That is what this video is about, with the source next to each step.
<short pause>

### 3 Solution
[SCREEN: Handbuch online, Abschnitt 4, der Wortlaut des Artikels 4 Absatz 1 als Zitat; Zoom auf die Quellenzeile wie in A11]
Step one: read the article as it stands since 27 July 2026. Article four, paragraph one:

[MARKER: Fact]
"Providers and deployers of AI systems shall take measures to support the development of AI literacy of their staff and other persons dealing with the operation and use of AI systems on their behalf, taking into account their technical knowledge, experience, education and training and the context the AI systems are to be used in, and considering the persons or groups of persons on whom the AI systems are to be used. This obligation does not require providers or deployers to guarantee any specific level of AI literacy of any individual."
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Art. 4 and Art. 113, point (a), consolidated version of 27 July 2026; Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, Art. 1, point (5) · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-23]

Three things stand in that text. Measures that support literacy. Prior knowledge and context are taken into account. And no guaranteed level for any person.

[SCREEN: Kommissionsseite „AI literacy, questions and answers“, die Antwort zur Dokumentation; Quellenzeile unten]
Step two: what counts as proof. The Commission's page of questions and answers on AI literacy says:

[MARKER: Fact]
"There is no need for a certificate. Organisations can keep an internal record of trainings and/or other guiding initiatives." The same page says that the persons covered are not only employees, but persons under the organisation's remit, such as a contractor, a service provider or a client. The page is guidance, not law.
[SOURCE: European Commission, AI literacy, questions and answers, page last updated 20 October 2026 · digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[MARKER: Interpretation]
Step three: our interpretation. The duty is narrower than it is often presented. It asks for measures that support literacy, considering what people know and where they use the tools. It prescribes no format, no minimum duration and no certificate. So the record you keep is a briefing record. It shows that a briefing took place, when, with whom and with which contents. It does not show that the briefing was sufficient, and no source we read says what sufficient is.

[SCREEN: Handbuch Abschnitt 4, Absatz „The briefing record“, die acht Inhalte]
Step four: hold the briefing. One sheet per briefing: date, duration, who conducted it, the form, the target group, the contents covered as a checklist, and the attendance list with name, function and signature. The eight contents mirror the policy: the policy itself, section by section; the permitted tools, and why private accounts are not used for work; what never goes into a tool, and the rule of doubt; two or three use cases from your own business; the typical failure patterns, such as invented figures and references, and what is checked before use; the reporting channel, and that a report has no consequences; when and how to label; and who is asked in case of doubt.

Step five: one briefing for everyone, or one per group.

[MARKER: Fact]
The article asks you to take into account the technical knowledge, experience, education and training of the persons, and the context the systems are to be used in.
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Art. 4(1) · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[MARKER: Interpretation]
Our interpretation: a briefing that is the same for everyone fits only if everyone does the same. In the example business, the office gets a longer briefing with use cases. The field staff get a shorter one on section one and section three of the policy: the permitted tools, and what never goes in.

[SCREEN: Handbuch Abschnitt 4, Absatz „The overview per person“]
Step six: the overview per person. One row per person who uses AI tools for work, including freelancers and contractors who work with your tools: function, first and last briefing, contents, the date they acknowledged the policy, the next briefing. Names are entered by you, never by the generator.

Step seven: two fields stay open.

[MARKER: Gap]
How often the briefing is repeated, and how long the record is kept: we found no source for this. Both fields are open on purpose. A new or changed tool is an obvious occasion to repeat; the rhythm is yours to set.

[SCREEN: Handbuch Abschnitt 6, die fünfzehn Schritte, Zeilen 8, 10, 11 und 15 hervorgehoben]
Step eight: make it count. Sign the policy with an effective date after the briefing, because a rule nobody knows is only a sheet of paper. Have the attendance list signed. Have each person confirm receipt of the policy, with the date in the overview. And on every new hire: briefing, acknowledgement, a new row.
<short pause>

### 4 Evidence
[SCREEN: A3, die vier Marker-Kacheln, Formen zeichnen sich]
Now the sources, with their markers: square for fact, diamond for our interpretation, half-circle for uncertain, dashed circle for a gap.

[SCREEN: Handbuch Abschnitt 4, erster Absatz, die Sätze zum 26. und 27. Juli 2026]
[MARKER: Fact]
Fact: Article four has applied since 2 February 2025. The amending Regulation of 8 July 2026 replaced the whole article with effect from 27 July 2026. Until 26 July 2026, the first sentence asked for measures "to ensure, to their best extent, a sufficient level of AI literacy". Since 27 July 2026, it asks for measures "to support the development of AI literacy", and the second sentence rules out a guaranteed level. A new paragraph two obliges the Commission and the member states to support providers and deployers, in particular small and medium-sized businesses; a new paragraph three asks the Board for recommendations.
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Art. 4 and Art. 113, point (a), consolidated version of 27 July 2026; Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, Art. 1, point (5) · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[SCREEN: Handbuch Abschnitt 4, Absatz zur Aufsicht]
[MARKER: Fact]
Fact: supervision. The Commission's page says supervision and enforcement apply from 2 August 2026. Article four is not in the catalogue of fines in Article ninety-nine, paragraphs three to five. The consequences follow the rules each member state adopts under Article ninety-nine, paragraph one.
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Art. 99(1), (3) to (5) and Art. 113, consolidated version of 27 July 2026 · retrieved 2026-10-19; European Commission, AI literacy, questions and answers, page last updated 20 October 2026 · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[SCREEN: Handbuch Abschnitt 7.2, Absatz zum KI-MIG; dann 7.1, der Kasten „Who supervises Article 4 and Article 50 in Austria“]
[MARKER: Fact]
In Germany, the act of 22 July 2026 names the Federal Network Agency as the authority, and it sets no fine for Article four.
[MARKER: Uncertain]
In Austria, this is open: as of 23 October 2026, no official source names the authority. The duty applies regardless, because the Regulation is directly applicable.
[SOURCE: Datenschutzbehörde (Austria), Künstliche Intelligenz und Datenschutz · dsb.gv.at · retrieved 2026-10-23; RIS, Federal Law Gazette, full-text search · retrieved 2026-10-23; KI-MIG of 22 July 2026 (BGBl. 2026 I No. 223), §§ 2, 6, 8, 15 · gesetze-im-internet.de · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[SCREEN: Handbuch Abschnitt 4, Absatz zu Hochrisiko-Systemen]
[MARKER: Fact]
Fact: high-risk systems need more. If a business uses an AI system for a purpose listed in Annex three, for example recruitment, selection, promotion, termination, task allocation or the monitoring of staff, the deployer must assign human oversight to persons with the necessary competence, training and authority. The Commission confirms that this training duty remains. Those high-risk rules apply from 2 December 2027. A briefing record does not cover that case, and section two of the policy excludes it.
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Art. 26(2), consolidated version of 27 July 2026 · retrieved 2026-10-19; European Commission, AI literacy, questions and answers, page last updated 20 October 2026 · retrieved 2026-10-23]
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Annex III, point 4; Art. 5(1), point (f); Art. 113, consolidated version of 27 July 2026 · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[SCREEN: Handbuch Abschnitt 4, letzter Absatz, zur Wirtschaftskammer]
[MARKER: Fact]
Fact: the Austrian Economic Chamber publishes free AI guidelines for small businesses, with a model policy. Its model paragraph nine suggests contents for training, from understanding AI and its fields of use to the technical limits of AI, legal aspects such as data protection, liability and copyright, and security. Useful. But the description of the duty on the same page reproduces the wording that applied until 26 July 2026, "ensure", and its date "since the end of 2025" was never right. Article four has applied since 2 February 2025, as the chamber's own page thirty-three says.
[SOURCE: Wirtschaftskammer Österreich, KI-Guidelines für KMU, 3rd edition, April 2026, pp. 25 to 26 and p. 33 · retrieved 2026-10-23; Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Art. 4 in the original version and in the consolidated version of 27 July 2026, and Art. 113 · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[SCREEN: Handbuch Abschnitt 7.1, erster Absatz]
[MARKER: Fact]
The guidelines predate the amending Regulation. Several of their pages say that the rules for high-risk systems apply from August 2026, while Article one hundred and thirteen now says 2 December 2027 for Annex three.
[SOURCE: Wirtschaftskammer Österreich, KI-Guidelines für KMU, 3rd edition, April 2026, pp. 5, 11, 14, 17, 20, 25, 26, 29, 33, 39 · wko.at · retrieved 2026-10-23; Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Art. 4 in the original version and in the consolidated version of 27 July 2026, Art. 50(1) and Art. 113, consolidated version of 27 July 2026 · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[MARKER: Interpretation]
Our interpretation: the model is well made. Use its wording where it fits you better. But whoever copies a sentence that names a deadline checks the date of the publication first, and the Regulation second.

[SCREEN: Handbuch Abschnitt 6, der Absatz „Step 5 is a pointer“ mit Halbkreis]
[MARKER: Uncertain]
This is open: whether an AI tool triggers the participation of a works council. That is national labour law. The German data protection authorities write that introducing an AI application will in some cases trigger co-determination. A business with a works council clarifies this with a specialist before procuring a tool.
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Art. 88(1) · retrieved 2026-10-23; Datenschutzkonferenz, Orientierungshilfe of 6 May 2024, section 1.12, p. 8 · retrieved 2026-10-18; Datenschutzkonferenz, Orientierungshilfe of 6 May 2024, section 2.2, p. 10 · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[SCREEN: Handbuch Abschnitt 8, Zeilen 8.9 und 8.10, gestrichelter Kreis]
[MARKER: Gap]
The gap, once more: repetition and retention. No source we read names either; the handbook lists this as open point eight point nine. Open point eight point ten sits next to it: Article four covers persons under the organisation's remit, so contractors are on the overview, but how a policy binds them is contract law, which we did not survey.
<short pause>

### 5 Example business
[SCREEN: Generator Toolkit 01 wie A9, Block H, Fragen H1 bis H5 mit vorbelegtem Beispielbetrieb; Bildschirmtext „Example Electrical Ltd · fictional“]
Back to the invented electrical contractor. In the generator, block H holds the five briefing questions. Who conducts the briefing: the owner. One briefing for everyone, or per group: per group, office and field staff. When: a date two weeks ahead. How often: the example chose "on every new tool and once a year". How long the records are kept: it chose five years. Those last two are the business's own decisions; the generator says so next to each, because no source names a minimum.

[SCREEN: Muster D4, example-electrical-ltd-training-en.pdf, Blatt „Office“: Kopf, Inhalte zum Ankreuzen, Teilnehmerliste; dann Blatt „field staff“; Fußzeile mit Aufbewahrung und nächster Einweisung]
What comes out is document D4, the briefing record. Two attendance sheets: one for the office, one for the field staff. Each sheet opens with the same sentence. It records that a briefing took place, when, with whom, with which contents; it does not record that it was sufficient. Below: date, conducted by, form, target group, policy version. Then the contents to tick, eight from the policy and one line for your own. Then the list: name, function, a box for "policy received and read", and the signature. At the bottom: how long the sheet is kept, and when the next briefing is due.

[SCREEN: Muster D4 Übersicht, example-electrical-ltd-training-overview-en.xlsx, Excel-Lesemodus, Spaltenköpfe; dann Blatt „Register of briefings“]
The second part is the overview per person, as a spreadsheet, with the columns from step six. A second sheet is a register of briefings: date, conducted by, form, duration, participants, target group, and the file name of the attendance list. The name column stays empty until you type the names; the generator never asks for them.

[SCREEN: Muster D6, example-electrical-ltd-plan-en.xlsx, Schritte 10, 11 und 15]
In the enactment plan, steps ten, eleven and fifteen carry exactly this: hold the briefing and have the list signed; have each person confirm receipt; brief every new hire and add the row.

### 6 What it is not
[SCREEN: Produktseite Toolkit 01, Absatz „What it is not“; dann Handbuch Abschnitt 8, Kopfzeile mit „eight remain open“]
What this is not. It is not legal advice, and no document claims that your business complies with a rule. The documents show what you decided, since when, for which tools, and who knows about it.

The briefing record shows that a briefing happened. It does not show that it was enough; no source we read says what enough is, and the sheet says so in its first lines. The handbook lists its open points in section eight: as of 23 October 2026, eight of seventeen remain open. For this topic: the rhythm and the retention period, a gap. Contractors and freelancers, contract law not surveyed. The works council, national law. And the authority in Austria, not yet named. You decide these, and the documents leave the fields open until you do.
<short pause>
