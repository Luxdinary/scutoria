### 2 Problem
[SCREEN: A12, Memo-Übersicht; dann die Karte „Does NIS2 apply to us?“ halten]
Take a fictional business: an electrical contractor in Austria, ten to forty-nine staff, no IT department, an external provider for the computers. No person and no business in this video is real. One morning a large customer sends a questionnaire; the covering email mentions NIS two, a deadline, and the words supply chain.

Two questions arrive at once, and they get mixed up. Does this law apply to us? And if not, why is the customer asking? Some pages say NIS two is only for large companies. Others say every supplier is affected. Both are half right, which does not help while a customer waits for a reply.

[SCREEN: Memo NIS2, Einstiegsseite mit dem Vorspann „Five questions on sector, size and special cases“]
The directive is clearer than the pages about it. It works with three things: the sector of the business, its size, and a short list of special cases. Those three decide who falls under it, together with the national law that transposes it. Who gets asked is a different matter: that follows from a duty the directive lays on your customer, not on you.
[SOURCE: Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 2(1) to (3) · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-19]

So the answer has two parts. Part one: check whether the law covers your business, with dated figures. Part two: understand what your customer must do, and answer measured by your size. Our free memo does part one in five questions; this video does part two.

<short pause>

### 3 Lösung
[SCREEN: Memo NIS2, Frage „Where is your business established?“, die vier Optionen]
Question one: where is your business established? Austria, Germany, another member state, or outside the Union with services in the Union. The directive sets the classification. The national law sets the deadline, the authority and the portal.
[SOURCE: European Commission, "NIS2 Directive transposition in EU countries", last update 1 July 2025; news of 7 May 2025 and 8 July 2026 · digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu · retrieved 2026-10-19; Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 41 · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[SCREEN: Frage „How large is your business…“, die drei Größenklassen mit ihren Hinweisen]
Question two: how large is your business, counting partner and linked enterprises of a group? The thresholds come from the EU definition of small and medium-sized enterprises. Fewer than fifty staff, and turnover or balance sheet total up to 10 million euros: micro or small. Fifty to two hundred and forty-nine staff: medium-sized. Two hundred and fifty or more, or turnover above 50 million and balance sheet above 43 million euros: large. The directive starts at medium-sized, and a business in a group adds the group's figures.
[SOURCE: Recommendation 2003/361/EC, Annex, Art. 2, 3 and 6 · retrieved 2026-10-19; Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 2(1) and recital 16 · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[MARKER: Interpretation]
Our interpretation: a business is medium-sized once it has fifty or more staff, or once turnover and balance sheet total are both above 10 million euros. The German and the Austrian law write the same thresholds, and both leave the group's figures out where the business is independent of its partner and linked enterprises as regards its network and information systems.
[SOURCE: BSI-Gesetz, § 28(1), (2) and (4) · gesetze-im-internet.de · retrieved 2026-10-23; NISG 2026, § 25(2) to (4) · ris.bka.gv.at · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[SCREEN: Frage „Which of these activities…“, Liste langsam bis „None of these“ scrollen]
Question three: which activities does your business carry out to a more than negligible extent? The list follows the two annexes. Annex one, the sectors of high criticality: energy, transport, banking, financial markets, health, water, digital infrastructure, managed IT services, public administration and space. Annex two: postal services, waste, chemicals, food, the manufacturing of medical devices, computers, electrical equipment, machinery and vehicles, digital providers and research. The last option is none of these: trade, crafts, retail, hospitality, consulting, construction, agriculture.
[SOURCE: Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 2(1) and (2), Annexes I and II · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-19; Commission Recommendation 2003/361/EC, Annex, Art. 2, 3 and 6 · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[SCREEN: Frage „Does any special case apply?“, die neun Kästchen]
Question four: does a special case apply? Some entities are covered regardless of size: public communications providers, trust service providers, top-level domain registries, DNS providers and registrars, and entities an authority has identified as critical or notified as essential or important. Two cases depend on a decision of the member state: the sole provider of a service essential for society or the economy, and a provider whose disruption would significantly affect the public. Ticking nothing is a complete answer.
[SOURCE: Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 2(1) to (3) · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[SCREEN: Frage „Do you supply products or services to businesses that are themselves covered…“, die drei Antworten]
Question five: do you supply businesses that are themselves covered, such as hospitals, utilities, banks, large manufacturers or public administration? Yes, no, or we do not know. This changes nothing in the classification. It changes what the memo tells you to prepare.

[SCREEN: Ergebnisseite, Lampe und Einstufung, der Grund mit dem Artikel]
From the five answers the memo derives one of four results. Likely yes, an essential entity. Likely yes, an important entity. Possibly, because a decision of the authority or a detail the memo cannot settle is missing. Or likely not directly in scope. In short: a large business of an Annex one type is essential; the other businesses of either annex from medium-sized upwards are important; below medium-sized, only the special cases apply.
[SOURCE: Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 3(1) and (2) · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[SCREEN: Ergebnisseite einer erfassten Einrichtung, der Befund zu den zehn Bereichen]
For an entity in scope, three things follow. Measures in at least ten areas, from risk analysis and incident handling to backup, supply chain security, training, access control and multi-factor authentication; the management approves them, attends training and can be held liable. Reporting of significant incidents: an early warning within 24 hours, a notification within 72 hours, a final report within one month. And registration with the national authority.
[SOURCE: Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 20, Art. 21(1) and (2), Art. 23(4) · retrieved 2026-10-19; Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 3(4) · retrieved 2026-10-19]

The memo does not decide for your business. It shows the five answers, the rule each triggers, and the step that follows. Keep the dated memo; it is the first page of your evidence file.

### 4 Beleg
[SCREEN: Ergebnisseite für Österreich, Befunde mit Markerformen und Quellenzeilen; auf die Quellenzeile zoomen wie in A11]
Now the deadlines, each with its marker.

[MARKER: Fact]
Austria. The NISG 2026 is in force in its core provisions since 1 October 2026. The authority is the new Federal Office for Cybersecurity. Covered entities register within three months through the NIS two services on the business service portal, that is by 1 January 2027. The authority names 4 January 2027 as the last day. The chamber of commerce names 31 December 2026; registering by then leaves no doubt. Within twelve months of the registration duty arising, each entity sends the authority a structured self-declaration, in particular on the security of its supply chains.
[SOURCE: NISG 2026, BGBl. I No. 94/2025, §§ 3a, 25, 29, 32, 33, 51; NISV 2026, BGBl. II No. 290/2026, § 2 · ris.bka.gv.at · retrieved 2026-10-19 and 2026-10-23; Bundesamt für Cybersicherheit, Registrierung (2 October 2026) · retrieved 2026-10-19; WKO, Fragen und Antworten zu NISG 2026 (8 October 2026) · wko.at · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[MARKER: Uncertain]
This is open: as of 19 October 2026 we found no official self-check tool on the authority's pages, so the assessment is the entity's own.
[SOURCE: NISG 2026, BGBl. I No. 94/2025, §§ 3a, 25, 29, 32, 33, 51; NISV 2026, BGBl. II No. 290/2026, § 2 · ris.bka.gv.at · retrieved 2026-10-19 and 2026-10-23; Bundesamt für Cybersicherheit, Registrierung (2 October 2026) · retrieved 2026-10-19; WKO, Fragen und Antworten zu NISG 2026 (8 October 2026) · wko.at · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[SCREEN: Ergebnisseite für Deutschland, der Fakt-Befund, dann der Unsicher-Befund zum 31. Juli 2026]
[MARKER: Fact]
Germany. The BSI Act of 2 December 2025 is in force since 6 December 2025. Essential entities are called besonders wichtige Einrichtungen there. Covered entities register with the BSI within three months of first qualifying. For entities covered since 6 December 2025 that deadline ended on 6 March 2026, and the BSI writes that the statutory period has passed. As of 30 September 2026, twenty thousand one hundred and forty-one entities had registered.
[SOURCE: BSI-Gesetz of 2 December 2025, BGBl. 2025 I No. 301, §§ 28, 30, 32, 33 · gesetze-im-internet.de · retrieved 2026-10-19 and 2026-10-23; BSI, NIS-2 in Zahlen (as of 30 September 2026), Registrierungspflicht · bsi.bund.de · retrieved 2026-10-19 and 2026-10-23]

[MARKER: Uncertain]
This is open: the BSI had allowed late registrations until 31 July 2026, and that period has also ended. We found the date only in secondary sources, not on the BSI pages.
[SOURCE: BSI, page "NIS-2-regulierte Unternehmen" · bsi.bund.de · retrieved 2026-10-23; the date of 31 July 2026 only in secondary sources (IHK Braunschweig, ZfK) · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[MARKER: Interpretation]
Our interpretation: if your business qualifies and has not registered, registering now is better than waiting, and the dated assessment belongs in your evidence file. The BSI's online self-check is a first pass; it does not replace your own assessment, and the BSI issues no binding decision.
[SOURCE: BSI-Gesetz of 2 December 2025, §§ 28, 30, 32, 33 · gesetze-im-internet.de · retrieved 2026-10-19, § 33(1) and (6) · retrieved 2026-10-23; BSI, "Registrierungspflicht" · retrieved 2026-10-23; BSI, FAQ, "NIS-2 in Zahlen", newsletter of 1 April 2026, press releases of 5 December 2025 and 6 January 2026 · bsi.bund.de · retrieved 2026-10-19; Bundesregierung, news of 8 December 2025 · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[SCREEN: Ergebnisseite für „Another EU member state“, der Unsicher-Befund]
[MARKER: Uncertain]
Other member states. The transposition deadline was 17 October 2024. The Commission's overview, last updated 1 July 2025, lists nineteen member states with a reasoned opinion of 7 May 2025, and on 8 July 2026 the Commission referred Ireland, Spain, France and the Netherlands to the Court of Justice. Which law, deadline, authority and portal apply in your member state, you check at the national source; the memo has verified Austria and Germany only.
[SOURCE: European Commission, "NIS2 Directive transposition in EU countries", last update 1 July 2025; news of 7 May 2025 and 8 July 2026 · digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu · retrieved 2026-10-19; Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 41 · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[MARKER: Uncertain]
A business established outside the Union is covered only if it provides services in the Union, and jurisdiction depends on the type of service; the memo cannot settle it.
[SOURCE: Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 2(1) and Art. 26 · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[MARKER: Fact]
The fines, once, as the directive writes them: for infringements of the measures or the reporting duties, at least 10 million euros or two percent of worldwide annual turnover for essential entities, at least 7 million euros or one point four percent for important entities, whichever is higher. They are written for entities in scope.
[SOURCE: Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 34(4) and (5) · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[SCREEN: Handbuch Toolkit 02 online, Abschnitt 1.1, der Fakt zu Art. 21 Abs. 2 Buchst. d mit Quellenzeile]
[MARKER: Fact]
Now the reason your customers ask anyway. Entities in scope must address supply chain security, including the relationships with their direct suppliers and service providers, and must take into account each supplier's vulnerabilities and the quality of its products and cybersecurity practices.
[SOURCE: Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 21(2), point (d), and Art. 21(3) · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[MARKER: Interpretation]
Our interpretation: that is the questionnaire. The directive does not tell the customer what to ask a supplier; it obliges the customer to consider the supplier's practices, and a questionnaire is the cheapest way to do that for hundreds of suppliers at once. The law binds the customer, not you; a supplier of an entity in scope is not in scope by that fact. What binds you is the contract that follows and, wherever you handle personal data, the GDPR, whose Article thirty-two requires appropriate technical and organisational measures.
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Art. 32(1)(a) to (d) · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[MARKER: Interpretation]
For an Austrian customer there is one more reason: whoever asks you in 2026 is collecting what it has to send to the authority in 2027. It does not make your answers a filing with the authority.
[SOURCE: BSI-Gesetz of 2 December 2025, BGBl. 2025 I No. 301, last amended on 23 July 2026, BGBl. 2026 I No. 226 · gesetze-im-internet.de · retrieved 2026-10-23; NISG 2026, BGBl. I No. 94/2025 of 23 December 2025, § 32(4), § 33(1) · ris.bka.gv.at · retrieved 2026-10-19]

### 5 Beispielbetrieb
[SCREEN: Memo NIS2 mit den Beispielantworten: Austria; fewer than 50 staff; None of these; keine Sonderfälle; Supplier yes; Bildschirmtext „Example Electrical Ltd · fictional“]
Back to the fictional electrical contractor. Country: Austria. Size: fewer than fifty staff, turnover and balance sheet total under 10 million euros, no parent company, so no group figures to add. Activities: the business installs electrical systems.
[SOURCE: Recommendation 2003/361/EC, Annex, Art. 2, 3 and 6 · retrieved 2026-10-19; Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 2(1) and recital 16 · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[MARKER: Interpretation]
Our interpretation: Annex two names the manufacturing of electrical equipment, not the trade that installs it. So the answer is none of these. Special cases: none. Supplier of covered entities: yes; the customer with the questionnaire is a hospital.
[SOURCE: Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 2(1) and (2), Annexes I and II · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-19; Commission Recommendation 2003/361/EC, Annex, Art. 2, 3 and 6 · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[SCREEN: Ergebnisseite: grüne Lampe, „Likely not directly in scope.“, der Grund, die drei Schritte]
The result: likely not directly in scope. The reason: no Annex one or Annex two type and no special case, Article two, paragraphs one and two. Below it, the finding on suppliers and three steps. Keep a dated note of this check with the figures and the sector, because customers and insurers ask for it, and repeat it when staff or turnover grow. Expect the questionnaire, and prepare dated answers on multi-factor authentication, backups, updates, access control and your incident contact.
[SOURCE: Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 2(2), points (b) to (e), and Art. 2(5) · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[SCREEN: Muster D5 Antwortschreiben, Seite mit der Abgleichstabelle, Spalte „Art. 21(2)“ zu den Abschnitten; dann A17, Muster D1 Übersichtstabelle]
Here the toolkit continues where the memo stops. The self-declaration D1 answers in nine sections with four honest answers per field: in place, partly, not yet with a date, not applicable with a reason. The cover letter D5 names the gaps with dates, and its table maps eighteen typical questions and the ten areas of Article twenty-one, paragraph two, to the sections of the self-declaration.
[SOURCE: Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 20, Art. 21(1) and (2), Art. 23(4) · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[MARKER: Interpretation]
Our interpretation: proportionality is written for the entity, not for its suppliers, but a customer that must justify its own measures by its size has a good reason to accept a small supplier's answers measured by the supplier's size.
[SOURCE: Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Art. 21(1) second subparagraph; Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690, Art. 2(2) second subparagraph and recital 5; NISG 2026, § 32(3); NISV 2026, § 4 · retrieved 2026-10-23]

### 6 Was es nicht ist
[SCREEN: A4, Fußzeile; dann Handbuch Toolkit 02, Abschnitt 6, Zeile 6.3 halten]
In the words of the toolkit: it is not legal advice, not an audit and not a certificate, and no document claims that your business is secure or complies with a law. The documents show what you have in place on a given date, what you will do by when, and who answers for it.

The open points, named rather than hidden. The day of the Austrian registration deadline differs between the authority and the chamber; the twelve months for the self-declaration count from the registration duty, and the chamber names 30 September 2027.
[SOURCE: NISG 2026, BGBl. I No. 94/2025, §§ 3a, 25, 29, 32, 33, 51; NISV 2026, BGBl. II No. 290/2026, § 2 · ris.bka.gv.at · retrieved 2026-10-19 and 2026-10-23; Bundesamt für Cybersicherheit, Registrierung (2 October 2026) · retrieved 2026-10-19; WKO, Fragen und Antworten zu NISG 2026 (8 October 2026) · wko.at · retrieved 2026-10-23]
