### 2 Problem
[SCREEN: Startseite EN, Satz unter dem Claim; langsam scrollen zu den zwei Angeboten]
The question arrives in three ways. A larger customer sends a security questionnaire and wants a signed answer with a date. An insurer's form asks what is in place. A new colleague asks what may go into the text assistant, and what may not.

Each time the question is the same: what applies here, since when, and who decided it. A paper in a drawer does not answer that. A dated document does: a date, a signature, a document number, and the open points listed next to the settled ones.

### 3 Solution
[SCREEN: Startseite EN, die zwei Angebote: Toolkit 01 blau, Toolkit 02 türkis; Bildschirmtext „Toolkit 01 · AI Policy“ und „Toolkit 02 · Security Evidence“]
Scutoria sells two toolkits. Toolkit one is the AI Policy Toolkit, the blue line: the rules for AI tools in your business. Toolkit two is the Security Evidence Toolkit, the turquoise line: the answer to the security questionnaire from your customer or insurer. Both work the same way. You answer questions, the generator writes seven dated documents and a handbook, and you sign, post, send and keep.

[SCREEN: A5, Produktseite Toolkit 01, „What you get“, langsam über D1 bis HB scrollen; Bildschirmtext „7 documents · 1 handbook“]
What you get in Toolkit one. An AI usage policy: nine sections from your answers, signed by the management. The one page to post: the rule of doubt, what never goes into a tool, what is checked, where incidents are reported; taken from the policy, so the two cannot contradict each other. A tool register with an approval form: one row per tool, seven questions per tool, and "unclear" is a finding, not a failure. A briefing record: an attendance list per group and an overview per person, the internal record the European Commission describes for Article four of the AI Act. A labelling checklist under Article fifty. An enactment plan with fifteen steps. And an evidence summary: one page, dated, with a document number, what is regulated, what is planned, what is open.
[SOURCE: scutoria.com, product page of the AI Policy Toolkit, as of 23 October 2026]
[SOURCE: European Commission, AI literacy, questions and answers, page last updated 20 October 2026 · digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu · retrieved 2026-10-23]

A cover letter that names the gaps with their dates, with a table that maps eighteen typical questions and the ten areas of Article twenty-one, paragraph two of the NIS two Directive to the sections of your self-declaration. A clause checklist for the security clauses customers write into contracts. And again the evidence summary, one dated page.

### 4 Evidence
[SCREEN: A3, die vier Marker-Kacheln, Formen zeichnen sich beim Scrollen]
Why can you show these documents? Because every rule in them has a reason in the handbook, and every reason has a source with its retrieval date. What is not settled is marked, not hidden. For this, every statement that matters carries one of four markers. The shape counts, never the colour alone.

[MARKER: Fact]
[SCREEN: A11, Handbuch Toolkit 01 online, eine Aussage mit Fakt-Marker, Zoom auf die Quellenzeile]
A filled square: Fact. Read at the original source, with retrieval date. An example: Article four of the AI Act, on AI literacy, applies since 2 February 2025.
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Art. 4 and Art. 113, point (a), consolidated version of 27 July 2026; Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, Art. 1, point (5) · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-23]

[MARKER: Interpretation]
[SCREEN: Handbuch Toolkit 01 online, Abschnitt 1, eine Aussage mit Raute]
A diamond: Interpretation. Our conclusion, open to argument. An example: neither the AI Act nor the GDPR expressly requires a written AI policy. Article four of the AI Act, Article twenty-four, paragraph one of the GDPR and the Trade Secrets Directive come closest. Our interpretation: these three are the reason for a written policy. The handbook marks that as interpretation, not as fact.
[SOURCE: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Art. 4; Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Art. 24(1); Directive (EU) 2016/943, Art. 2(1)(c) · EUR-Lex · retrieved 2026-10-18 and 2026-10-19]

[MARKER: Uncertain]
[SCREEN: Handbuch Toolkit 02 online, Abschnitt 2.2, Kasten zur Passwortregel, halber Kreis]
A half circle: Uncertain. Sources contradict each other or the law is open. An example from Toolkit two: the Implementing Regulation under NIS two requires password changes at fixed intervals. NIST advises against periodic changes without suspicion, and the German BSI argued publicly against forced changes. This is open: the handbook shows both positions and says what was checked and when. Your business decides.
[SOURCE: NIST SP 800-63B-4, July 2025, sections 3.1.1.1 and 3.1.1.2 · retrieved 2026-10-19; BSI press releases of 31 January 2025 and 30 January 2026 · retrieved 2026-10-19; Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690, Annex 11.6.2 · retrieved 2026-10-19]

[MARKER: Gap]
[SCREEN: Handbuch Toolkit 01 online, Abschnitt 8, Zeile 8.9, gestrichelter Kreis]
A dashed circle: Gap. No source answers it, so you decide and the field stays open on purpose. An example: how often the briefing is repeated and how long the record is kept. We found no source for this. The document leaves the field open, and your business enters its own rhythm.
[SOURCE: Scutoria, handbook of the AI Policy Toolkit, section 8, point 8.9, version of 23 October 2026]
