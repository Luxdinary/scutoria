EN (198 Wörter):
Four shapes tell you how much a sentence is worth. In every Scutoria handbook, every statement that matters carries one of four markers, and the shape counts, never the colour alone. A filled square: Fact. Read at the original source, with retrieval date. A diamond: Interpretation. Our conclusion, open to argument. A half circle: Uncertain. Sources contradict each other or the law is open. A dashed circle: Gap. No source answers it, so you decide and the field stays open on purpose.
[SOURCE: scutoria.com, product page of the AI Policy Toolkit, as of 23 October 2026]
One example of a gap: how often the briefing is repeated and how long the record is kept. We found no source for this, so the document leaves the field open and your business enters its own rhythm.
[SOURCE: Scutoria, handbook of the AI Policy Toolkit, section 8, point 8.9, version of 23 October 2026]
What this does for you: when a customer or an insurer asks why a rule says what it says, you point at the marker and the source next to it. What is open is listed, not hidden. One step today: open a handbook online, pick any paragraph and look for the shape next to it. The whole walk is in the video linked below. The handbooks are free on scutoria.com. EN (206 Wörter):
Why the business in the generator is not real. Both Scutoria toolkits open with the answers of an example business: an electrical contractor with ten to forty-nine staff in Austria. It is fictional, and no real person appears in it.
[SOURCE: scutoria.com, product page of the Security Evidence Toolkit, as of 23 October 2026]
Why prefill at all? Because an empty form is the hardest page to start on. With the example in place, you read an answer that could be yours and change only what differs. Why fictional? Because a real business would carry real names, real gaps and a real customer, and none of that belongs in a sample that everyone can download. And why this business? Because its picture is realistic rather than tidy. In the security toolkit, its answers say: multi-factor authentication on email yes, on the cloud console not yet; a daily backup, but no copy out of reach; a restore never tested; no emergency plan. The handbook calls it a realistic mixed picture, and the documents show how to say it with a date.
[SOURCE: Scutoria, handbook of the Security Evidence Toolkit, section 0, version of 23 October 2026]
One step today: download the sample self-declaration and compare it with your own picture, field by field. The whole walk is in the video linked below. The samples are free on scutoria.com. Synthetic voice, sources on screen, no legal advice.

Warum der Betrieb im Generator nicht echt ist. Beide Scutoria-Toolkits beginnen mit den Antworten eines Beispielbetriebs: ein Elektrotechnikbetrieb mit zehn bis neunundvierzig Beschäftigten in Österreich. Er ist erfunden, und keine echte Person kommt darin vor.
[QUELLE: scutoria.com, Produktseite des Sicherheitsnachweis-Toolkits, Stand 23. Oktober 2026]

Weil ein echter Betrieb echte Namen, echte Lücken und einen echten Kunden hätte, und nichts davon gehört in ein Muster, das jeder herunterladen kann. Und warum dieser Betrieb? Weil sein Bild realistisch ist und nicht aufgeräumt. Im Sicherheitsnachweis-Toolkit sagen seine Antworten: Multi-Faktor-Authentifizierung bei E-Mail ja, bei der Cloud-Konsole noch nicht; tägliche Sicherung, aber keine Kopie außer Reichweite; Wiederherstellung nie geprobt; kein Notfallplan. Das Handbuch nennt das ein realistisches Mischbild, und die Dokumente zeigen, wie man es mit Datum sagt.
